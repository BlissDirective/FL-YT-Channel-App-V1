# Channel Concept Review — 4 Candidates (2026)

A deep review, expansion, and ranking of four candidate channel concepts for the
studio. Scored on the same six levers as
[preliminary-niche-research.md](../preliminary-niche-research.md): **RPM/CPM**,
**watch-time/retention**, **search + evergreen demand**, **faceless
automatability**, **licensed-source fit**, and **policy/monetization safety** —
plus one 2026-specific lever that now sits over all of them:

> **The July 2025 "inauthentic content" rule.** YouTube renamed its repetitious
> content policy and now demonetizes *mass-produced, low-insight* content.
> AI-as-a-tool is explicitly fine; what's required is **original value —
> commentary, analysis, storytelling, education.** This is decisive for ranking:
> it *rewards* the studio's model (Claude script + QC gate + human approval =
> genuine editorial POV) and *punishes* thin, shock-bait auto-generation. Every
> concept below is graded partly on how naturally it produces original insight.

*Numbers are illustrative US bands, not promises — RPM swings with geography,
season, and audience. Validate each live with **Scout** + **Run intelligence**
before committing (see the bottom of this doc).*

## TL;DR ranking

| Rank | Concept | One-line verdict | RPM band |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Investing in the AI Economy** | Highest economic ceiling — finance RPM × the timeliest demand story on the platform, with a genuinely under-served angle. | **★★★★★ ~$15–30** |
| **2** | **The Past, Present & Future of Space Exploration** | Safest, cheapest, most evergreen — and the *best* fit for the studio's free NASA/gov source library. The reliability anchor. | ★★★ ~$4–12 |
| **3** | **Analyzing Movie Tech** | The most *ownable* concept with the biggest episodic+evergreen content surface; carries a manageable IP-sourcing constraint the repo already has a playbook for. | ★★★☆ ~$6–14 |
| **4** | **Science & Tech: The Dark Side** | Highest viral/retention upside, but the "shock/horror" framing is the one concept that fights both the RPM ceiling *and* advertiser-friendly + inauthentic-content policy. Winnable, but needs the most editorial discipline. | ★★★ ~$4–12 |

**Two rankings, one note.** The table above is **RPM/durability-weighted**. If you
instead weight for **raw reach/virality**, concepts 2 and 4 climb (cosmic awe and
"dark side" curiosity are the strongest CTR engines here) — but reach at a
lower, more monetization-fragile RPM is exactly the trade the portfolio model is
meant to hedge. See *Suggested portfolio* at the end.

## 1. Investing in the AI Economy — *the RPM anchor* 🏆

**The pitch (yours, sharpened):** the channel for the curious investor who wants
to understand the AI boom deeply enough to make confident decisions — closing the
gap between surface-level hype videos and impenetrable analyst jargon.

This is the standout. It sits at the **intersection of the two highest-CPM ad
categories on YouTube** — personal finance/investing ($15–45 CPM) *and* AI/tech,
a surging advertiser segment — so it inherits finance economics while riding the
single biggest demand story on the platform.

- **RPM:** ★★★★★ (~$15–30, peaks higher). Brokerage, fintech, and AI-tool
  advertisers bid hard; lifetime customer value is huge.
- **Retention:** ★★★★ — your stated angle *is* a retention strategy. "Hype vs.
  jargon" framing → explainer/"one concept fully explained" format, which retains
  far better than thin list content.
- **Demand:** ★★★★★ — timely (AI capex, Nvidia/Rubin, hyperscaler spend are
  front-page) *and* evergreen where you teach durable mental models ("how to
  value a company riding a capex supercycle," "what a moat actually is in AI").
- **Faceless:** ★★★★★ — charts, data-viz, stock b-roll, screen-recording, calm
  authoritative VO. Among the cheapest formats to produce well.
- **Source fit:** ★★★★ — generated charts + Pexels b-roll + company **press-kit**
  logos/imagery (compliant lane). No archival dependency.
- **Policy:** ⚠️ **YMYL is the #1 caution.** Educate, never give individualized
  buy/sell advice. This is *the* concept where the QC agent's policy check earns
  its keep. Frame every video as "here's the framework / here's how to think,"
  not "buy this." Done right, it's safe; done lazily, it's a strike magnet.
- **Competition:** finance is the most crowded niche on YouTube — **but** generic
  "personal finance" is what's saturated. *AI-economy investor education,
  explicitly bridging hype and jargon,* is a sharper, newer, thinner wedge. This
  is the textbook "win on a sharper angle" play.

**Expanded brief (drop straight into the project wizard / `update_project`):**
- **Niche:** AI-Economy Investing (education)
- **Audience:** Curious retail investors who believe in the AI shift but are stuck
  between hype reels and analyst jargon, and want to form their *own* convictions.
- **Angle:** Plain-English, framework-first investor education on the AI economy —
  no tickers-to-the-moon, no jargon wall.
- **Tone:** `authoritative` + `calm`
- **Thumbnail style:** `conceptual` / `bold-text` · **Palette:** Emerald or Amber
- **Format ladder:** "One AI concept, fully explained" (evergreen) · "How to read
  [Nvidia/hyperscaler] earnings" · post-mortems of past tech bubbles vs. today ·
  "the business model behind [AI company]."
- **Guardrail:** every script ends as education, not a recommendation. Set the
  project **RPM to the finance band** in Settings so revenue estimates are real.

## 2. The Past, Present & Future of Space Exploration — *the reliability anchor*

**The pitch (yours):** travel through space *and* time — futuristic space
concepts, plus ranking/reviewing past space events and tech.

The lowest-risk bet of the four and the **single best fit for this studio's
economics.** Space content runs almost entirely on **NASA/gov public-domain
footage** — which is free, abundant, gorgeous, and *already wired into the Source
Library.* Near-zero footage cost + spotless licensing.

- **RPM:** ★★★ (~$4–12). The trade-off for everything else being excellent.
- **Retention:** ★★★★ — cosmic awe is one of the strongest retention engines on
  the platform (Kurzgesagt ~21M, Astrum ~2.6M, Event Horizon ~1.5M prove the
  ceiling). Your past/present/future structure is a natural series spine.
- **Demand:** ★★★★ — perennial and deeply evergreen. "Rank the boldest space
  missions ever," "the science of a Mars transit," "what a Dyson swarm would
  actually take" age slowly and re-surface for years.
- **Faceless:** ★★★★★ — VO + NASA/ESA footage + animation. Textbook faceless.
- **Source fit:** ★★★★★ **(best of the four).** NASA/gov + Wikimedia + Archive are
  public domain and pre-integrated. This is the concept the studio is *most* built
  to run cheaply.
- **Policy:** ★★★★★ **(safest of the four).** No YMYL, no shock, family- and
  advertiser-friendly, clean public-domain provenance.
- **Competition:** established big players, but proven, expandable demand. Your
  "time-travel through space history + futurism + ranking" framing is a workable
  wedge against pure-explainer incumbents.

**Expanded brief:**
- **Niche:** Space Exploration — past, present & future
- **Audience:** Awe-seekers and armchair futurists who want the cosmos explained
  with cinematic visuals and a sense of journey, not a lecture.
- **Angle:** Travelling space *and* time — ranking the past, explaining the
  present, and seriously imagining the future of spaceflight.
- **Tone:** `curious` + `aspirational` · **Thumbnail:** `cinematic` ·
  **Palette:** Lavender
- **Format ladder:** "Ranking every [X] mission" (evergreen list-essay) · "The
  real science of [futuristic concept]" · "What [past event] actually changed."
- **Edge:** lowest cost-per-video in the portfolio → highest margin at a given
  view count. The dependable compounding asset.

## 3. Analyzing Movie Tech — *the most ownable concept*

**The pitch (yours):** review the tech from sci-fi/fantasy films — e.g. the
neural-link from *Avatar* — assessing feasibility, risks, and *if/when* it could
exist. A "massive content option" across every new sci-fi release.

The most **differentiated and structurally ownable** idea of the four, and you're
right about the content surface: it's both **evergreen** (a deep back-catalog —
*Avatar*, *Iron Man*'s JARVIS, *Minority Report*, *Black Mirror*, *Dune*) **and
episodic** (every new sci-fi/fantasy release is a fresh, pre-marketed content
peg). A repeatable segment structure — *What the film shows → Where the real
science is → Feasibility & timeline → Risks* — is a format you can *own*, and it
generates exactly the original analysis the inauthentic-content policy rewards.

- **RPM:** ★★★☆ (~$6–14). Better than pure entertainment because the *payload* is
  science/tech/AI — it pulls tech advertisers while keeping an entertainment-sized
  audience. A nice RPM-vs-reach middle ground.
- **Retention:** ★★★★ — a built-in hook (a film people already love) plus a
  curiosity-gap payoff ("is it real? when?"). High CTR per title.
- **Demand:** ★★★★ — evergreen catalog + a perpetual stream of release-driven
  spikes. Effectively self-refilling.
- **Faceless:** ★★★★ — VO + AI concept renders + real science b-roll + diagrams.
- **Source fit:** ⚠️ **the one real constraint.** You **cannot** cut film footage
  into renders. This is precisely the IP problem the repo already solves in
  [gaming-source-lanes.md](../gaming-source-lanes.md): carry videos with **real
  science footage, AI concept art, diagrams, generic b-roll, and press-kit
  stills**, treat the film itself as *commentary/criticism reference* (link, don't
  redistribute). It's an analysis/commentary channel, which leans fair-use — but
  that's a per-video operator judgment, not something the app grants.
- **Policy:** mostly safe (educational/transformative commentary); **copyright/IP
  is the standing caution** — manage it with the compliant lanes, never scraped
  clips.
- **Competition:** "the science of [movie]" exists but isn't saturated; the
  systematic *feasibility + timeline + risk* treatment is a strong, brandable
  upgrade over one-off takes.

**Expanded brief:**
- **Niche:** Sci-Fi Movie Tech — feasibility & futurism
- **Audience:** Sci-fi fans and tech-curious viewers who finish a film asking
  "could we actually build that?" and want a credible, non-dry answer.
- **Angle:** Treating fictional technology as a serious engineering/feasibility
  question — what's real, what's missing, and when it could arrive.
- **Tone:** `curious` + `authoritative` · **Thumbnail:** `conceptual` /
  `cinematic` · **Palette:** Lavender or Amber
- **Format ladder:** fixed 4-beat segment (show → science → feasibility/timeline →
  risks) per technology; "ranking the most/least plausible tech in [film]";
  release-tie-in episodes.
- **Guardrail:** press-kit/own-capture + AI concept art only; the film is
  *referenced*, never re-uploaded. Keep the attribution ledger clean.

## 4. Science & Tech: The Dark Side — *highest upside, highest discipline cost*

**The pitch (yours):** the dark side of science & tech — scary/shocking
technologies, unsettling experiments, shocking theories. Curiosity, shock,
*moderate horror*, big science, big excitement.

The most **viral** concept here — dread and curiosity are elite CTR/retention
fuel, and "things they don't want you to know about [tech/experiment]" is a
proven binge engine. But it's also the **only concept that fights three of our
six levers at once**, which is why it ranks fourth *despite* the upside.

- **RPM:** ★★★ (~$4–12), and **realistically pressured downward.** "Shocking,"
  "horror," and "disturbing" framings trigger YouTube's *advertiser-friendly*
  guidelines — the yellow-icon / limited-ads risk — which caps effective RPM below
  even the science average.
- **Retention:** ★★★★★ — the standout strength. Shock + curiosity hooks are the
  best in the group for CTR and binge watch-time.
- **Demand:** ★★★★ — huge appetite for "dark/forbidden science" content,
  reasonably evergreen.
- **Faceless:** ★★★★ — AI visuals, archival, stock, dramatic VO.
- **Source fit:** ★★★★ — Wikimedia/Archive/stock/AI-generated cover it well.
- **Policy:** ⚠️⚠️ **the highest-risk concept, on two fronts.** (1)
  *Advertiser-friendly content* — "moderate horror" and shock can demonetize or
  limit ads; the safe center of gravity is **shocking *science*, not gore.** (2)
  *Inauthentic-content* — sensational + AI-generated is exactly the silhouette the
  July 2025 rule scrutinizes, so each video **must** be carried by genuine
  scientific substance and original analysis, not vibes. The studio's QC + human
  gate is what keeps this side of the line — but it's the concept that leans on
  them hardest.

**Expanded brief (with the risk dialed down to "winnable"):**
- **Niche:** The unsettling edge of science & technology
- **Audience:** Curiosity-seekers who love the thrill of "wait, that's real?" —
  drawn by unease but staying for genuine science.
- **Angle:** The genuinely unsettling frontier of science/tech, taken
  *seriously* — real experiments, real theories, real implications, dramatized
  but never fabricated.
- **Tone:** `alarming` shading to `curious` (lead with the hook, **land on the
  science**) · **Thumbnail:** `dramatic` · **Palette:** Coral or Lavender
- **Format ladder:** "the experiment that [unsettling result]" · "the theory that
  breaks your intuition about [X]" · "the technology nobody's ready for."
- **Guardrail:** *shocking, not graphic.* Substance over sensation on every
  video, or RPM and monetization both erode. If you tone the brand toward
  "mind-blowing science" rather than "horror," this concept's score rises
  materially — worth A/B testing the positioning with Scout before committing.

## Suggested portfolio

These four split cleanly into one RPM anchor + a cheap, synergistic "wonder"
cluster:

- **Anchor (RPM):** **#1 AI-Economy Investing** — highest ceiling, run it first
  and set the niche RPM to the finance band.
- **Volume (cheap, safe, evergreen):** **#2 Space Exploration** — lowest
  cost-per-video, cleanest policy, best source fit. The dependable compounder.
- **Synergy bonus:** #2, #3, and #4 share one production stack (futuristic/science
  visuals, AI concept art, NASA + Archive + stock, dramatic-to-curious VO) and the
  *same* compliant source lanes. You can run any two of them at near-marginal cost
  once the first is dialed in — they're closer to sub-brands of one "science &
  future" engine than three separate builds. #3 Movie Tech is the most
  differentiated of the cluster; #4 the most viral but the most policy-sensitive.

A strong first move: **launch #1 (AI-Economy) as the anchor and #2 (Space) as the
volume play**, then add #3 or #4 once the shared science-visual pipeline is proven.

## Validate before you commit (in-app)

For each finalist, run the studio's own validation loop rather than trusting these
bands:

1. **Scout chat** on a draft project: *"tear down the top channels in [concept]"*
   and *"what proven topics am I missing?"* — pulls real titles + view counts.
2. **Run intelligence** to land scored, repurposable idea cards from the live niche
   so you can judge real demand and angle headroom. (Intelligence searches on niche
   *keywords*, so phrase the niche concretely.)
3. Set each project's **niche RPM** in Settings to the band above so estimated
   revenue is realistic from day one.
4. For #1 lean on the **QC policy check** (YMYL); for #3/#4 confirm the **compliant
   source lanes** carry the visuals before scaling.

*A research-grounded ranking and a starting hypothesis — confirm each with live
Scout + intelligence data before launching.*
