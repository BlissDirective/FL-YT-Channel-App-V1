# Tiny Worlds — Channel Bible

**Recommended channel name:** **Thimble Town** (tagline: *"Life in the tiny worlds"*). The working title "Tiny Worlds" is kept as a franchise umbrella. See §3.
**Concept:** Miniature diorama universes told through gentle "life in the tiny world" stories. A mouse runs a ramen shop, a snail lights the lamps and a wren carries the post. The look is tilt-shift macro with a warm palette, and the sound is ASMR foley. The main track is wordless, with an optional soft-narrated series.
**Pipeline:** SOUL Standard keyframe per section → Higgsfield Seedance 2.5 i2v (default) or Cinema Studio 4.0 (establishing shots) → optional ElevenLabs narration → Remotion assembly.
**Research date:** 2026-09-27. `[U]` = unverified or estimated. Every other claim is cited in the Sources list (§10).

## 1. Niche breakdown and 2026 market trends

### 1.1 Cozy/comfort, miniature/diorama, tilt-shift, AI-miniature ASMR
- **Cozy is a mainstream demand signal, not a fad.** YouTube's own culture blog frames cozy as comfort plus warmth plus self-care, and says the community is growing and widening into analog hobbies [S1]. Steam's "cozy" keyword surged roughly 675%, with more than 1,400 cozy-tagged titles by early 2026 [S2]. Epidemic Sound and Think with Google both flag escapism and relaxed, meandering storytelling as 2026 YouTube trends [S3][S4].
- **Miniature diorama worlds are a named 2026 3D-design trend.** They appear as looping cozy tabletop scenes and miniature cafés across Shorts, TikTok and Instagram. The format suits short-form because it is easy to watch and share [S5].
- **AI ASMR is one of the fastest-growing short-form categories.** It "exploded" in late 2025 and includes Mini World ASMR, tiny food and tiny workers [S6][S7]. One vendor blog says growing AI ASMR channels reached their first 10K followers in 3–5 weeks. That is a vendor claim, so treat it as [U] [S7]. Tool vendors now sell "AI miniature / tiny world" templates, which means the low end will be flooded [S8]. **Our moat is story, recurring characters and craft. Novelty is not a moat.**
- **Tilt-shift heritage.** Keith Loutit pioneered tilt-shift time-lapse miniaturisation in 2008. His insight still holds: *motion matters as much as optics*, so miniature illusions need slightly sped-up, toy-like movement, not only blur [S9]. We apply that insight through a style rule (§4.1).

### 1.2 Tiny kitchen / miniature cooking / cozy room, and the lo-fi crossover
- **Miniature cooking is proven and large.** Miniature Space held the Guinness record for most-subscribed miniature cooking channel and is now about 3.2M [S10][S11]. Tastemade's *Tiny Kitchen* drew "hundreds of millions of views" [S12]. Miniature Cusina (about 1.5M–2.9M depending on source) explicitly pairs tiny cooking with ASMR and childhood nostalgia [S11][S13].
- **The gap in that niche** is that it is all real hands and real food, with no characters and no world. Nobody owns *the residents* of the tiny world. We do not compete on "edible, real". We compete on "a place you visit and people you know".
- **Cozy room and lo-fi.** Lofi Girl has about 15.85M subscribers as of Sept 2026 [S14]. Animated cozy-ambience channels such as Cozy Rain (about 734K) sell ambience files on Gumroad [S15][S16]. Lo-fi viewers already accept a *looping animated place* as background. A tiny ramen shop in the rain is that format with a story on top.
- **Crossover play.** Every long episode gets a *Night Lights* sleep/ambient cut (1–3h, §8). That cut serves the lo-fi and ambience audience, adds watch hours, and is built from existing clips.

### 1.3 Global/wordless reach, RPM, sponsors, merch
- **Wordless means zero localisation.** Titles and descriptions get auto-translated metadata, and the optional narrated series can be dubbed with ElevenLabs later.
- **RPM, honestly:** this is a lower-RPM niche. Relax, sleep and ASMR content is often quoted at **$0.50–$2.50 RPM** [S17]. Other trackers give ambient and nature content **$2–5** [S17]. All-niche medians are around $2.30, against education at about $10.22 [S17]. Kids-designated content earns far less (§9.3).
  - **Planning assumption:** long-form **$1.50–3.50 RPM** `[U]`, Shorts **$0.03–0.08 per 1K** `[U]`, and sleep cuts at the low end but with very long sessions.
  - **Cost reality:** a 10-min episode costs about 600s × $0.2057 = **$123 list** in video generation alone. Break-even at $2.50 RPM is about **50K views per episode** `[U]`. Economics therefore depend on (a) each long episode being re-used for 3–5 Shorts plus one sleep cut, (b) sponsors and (c) merch/digital goods. Do not rely on AdSense alone.
- **Sponsor fit, best to worst:**
  1. Cozy/indie games. The genre is crowded and publishers are fighting for discovery, so they need exactly this audience [S2].
  2. Stationery, journaling and craft/miniature kits.
  3. Meditation and sleep apps.
  4. Tea, coffee and ramen DTC brands.
  5. Cozy-lamp and home brands.

  Integration style is a 10–15s "shop window" placement: the product appears as a tiny sign or crate inside the world, plus a description link. Never use a spoken hard sell on the wordless track.
- **Merch and digital goods, which are high potential:**
  - Character plush (Miso, Lumen).
  - Enamel pins.
  - Downloadable "Night Lights" ambience files, following the Cozy Rain/Gumroad model [S16].
  - Phone wallpapers, a printable paper-diorama kit of Miso's shop, and a calendar of Tiny Seasons.
  - Longer-term: a licensing pitch for a cozy game or a picture book `[U]`.

**What wins, by length:**
- **Long (5–15 min ambient story):**
  - One place and one time arc (dawn→night, or a build start→opening night).

  - A reveal payoff near 70–80% (the shop's lanterns glow, the snow globe is finished).

  - A soft music bed under foggy, detailed foley.
  - Chapter markers named like a storybook ("4:10 — The first customer").
- **Short (60s–4 min, vertical):**
  - One satisfying process or one micro-story.
  - Hook in frame 1: extreme macro of a glowing, steaming or moving thing.
  - Scale reveal by 5–8s (pull back: it's a *thimble*).
  - Payoff by 70%, then a seamless loop back to frame 1 (see end_image_url, §5.3).

## 4. Unified style bible

### 4.1 Look: tilt-shift/macro miniature realism
- **Medium:** hand-built miniature diorama. Found-object architecture: teacup houses, a thimble water tower, a spool café table, matchbox sheds and a tin-can lighthouse. Surfaces are painted wood, felt, clay, paper and real micro-props. **Signature rule: every set includes at least one recognisable everyday object used as architecture.** That object is our scale cue and our visual trademark.
- **Characters:** needle-felted and hand-painted resin figurines about 2–4 cm tall. They are visibly *miniature craft* and never photoreal animals.
- **Optics:**
  - Macro 60–100mm look and shallow DOF (f/2.8 equivalent at macro distance).
  - Tilt-shift focal band across the subject, with strong falloff top and bottom.
  - Slight bokeh "fairy lights" from practicals.
  - Grain: fine 35mm. No vignette over 15%.
- **Scale cues in every establishing shot:**
  - Visible fibres in the felt.
  - Paint brush marks.
  - Oversized dust motes in light shafts.
  - A giant everyday object at the edge of frame (a pencil, a mug rim, a fern leaf).
- **Motion (after Loutit):** residents move slightly *toy-like*: small steps and gentle bobbing. Steam and water move at real speed, because they are real at our scale, and that contrast sells the miniature.

### 4.2 Palette (hex)
| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Lantern Amber | `#F2B35E` | Practical lamp glow, window light, the key brand colour |
| Honey | `#E8A94B` | Golden-hour key light, wood highlights |
| Terracotta | `#C8643B` | Roofs, Miso's apron, warm accents |
| Cream Linen | `#F6EAD3` | Paper, steam, highlights, thumbnail text |
| Sage Moss | `#9DB28A` | Foliage, Bramble's cardigan |
| Walnut | `#6B4A33` | Wood, shadows in warm scenes |
| Dusk Plum | `#5B4A6B` | Twilight sky, shadow fill |
| Night Ink | `#2E3A55` | Night exteriors (never pure black) |
| Rain Slate | `#7C8C99` | Wet stone, rain days |
| Snow Blush | `#EFE3E6` | Winter highlights |

**Rules:**
- Warm/cool ratio of about 70/30.
- Every night scene contains at least one Lantern Amber source.
- No neon, no saturated primaries and no pure black or white.

### 4.3 Lighting
- **Golden hour** is the default for exteriors: a low 15–25° key from camera-left in Honey.
- **Practicals do the storytelling.** Lamps inside tiny windows, string lights, a stove glow and lantern posts all motivate the light.
- **Blue hour (Dusk Plum/Night Ink) plus amber windows** is the money shot, and it is the channel's ident moment.
- Soft haze and volumetric shafts are allowed at low density.

### 4.4 Recurring tiny residents (LOCKED — paste verbatim into every keyframe prompt that features them)
```
MISO — a small field-mouse ramen cook, needle-felted figurine texture, warm fawn-cream fur,
pale pink round ears, tiny black bead eyes, a navy-blue cloth headband (hachimaki) tied at
the back, a rust-terracotta half apron over a cream linen shirt with rolled sleeves, a white
cloth tucked in the apron string. About 3 cm tall. Calm, precise, kind. Runs "Miso's
Noodle Stand" on Noodle Row.

BRAMBLE — an elderly hedgehog baker and tea-keeper, hand-painted resin figurine, soft
brown-grey quills with cream tips, round gold wire spectacles, a sage-green knit cardigan
with two wooden buttons, a flour-dusted cream apron. About 3.5 cm tall. Slow, warm,
grandmotherly. Runs "Bramble's Bakery & Tea" in a teapot-shaped building.

LUMEN — a snail lamplighter, glossy clay shell in honey-amber with a cream spiral, soft
sage-grey body, a tiny brass lantern hanging from a hook on the shell, a small navy peaked
cap. About 2.5 cm long. Unhurried. Lights every street lamp at dusk (the channel ident).

WREN — a small wren postbird courier, felted chestnut-brown feathers with fine cream

```
**Consistency rules:**
- Never change outfits except for seasonal overlays. Seasonal overlays are additive only: a scarf, a raincoat or a knit hat, each in palette colours.
- Residents never speak on camera. They express themselves through posture, small gestures and props.
- Supporting walk-ons (customers, travellers) are unnamed: frogs, beetles, voles or sparrows, in the same figurine medium. **Unnamed walk-ons rotate so no episode is "the same cast doing the same thing".**

### 4.5 Camera grammar
| Move | Use | Speed |
|---|---|---|
| **Macro dolly** (lateral, 1–3 cm/s at scale) | Shop-front reveals, following Lumen down the lane | Very slow, constant |
| **Slow push-in** | Emotional beats: steam over a bowl, a lit window | 8–15s per push |
| **Top-down build** (90° overhead) | Build sequences, tile/plank assembly | Locked or slow rotate |
| **Rack focus** (foreground prop → character) | Section openers, scale reveals | 2–3s rack |
| **Pull-back scale reveal** | Hooks: "it's inside a teacup" | 4–6s |
| **Crane-up establishing** | Town wides at golden or blue hour (Cinema Studio) | Calm |
| **"Giant hand" moments** (optional, max 1 per episode) | A human fingertip places a final prop, or a warm hand shelters the town from rain | Soft and slow. Hands are stylised and slightly soft-focus. Never the protagonist. |
| **Forbidden** | Whip pans, handheld shake, fast zooms, Dutch angles | — |

### 4.6 Season and weather system
- **Episode clock:** each world has a time-of-day arc and the channel has a **real-calendar season**. Publish Tiny Seasons in sync: Spring in March–May, Summer in June–August, Autumn in September–November, Winter in December–February.
- **Weather states (one per episode):**
  - Clear golden.
  - Soft rain (tin-roof drip foley).
  - Fog morning.
  - First snow.
  - Windy leaf-fall.
  - Thunderstorm (distant, never scary).
  - Heat-haze summer night.
  - Starry clear.
- **Seasonal overlays:**
  - Spring: blossom confetti and green shoots.
  - Summer: fireflies, open windows and awnings.
  - Autumn: acorn caps, leaf drifts and pumpkin lanterns.
  - Winter: cotton-wool snow, frosted windows and knit wear.
- **Continuity canon** (tracked in a show-bible spreadsheet): shop names, signage, what Miso serves each season, Lumen's route, and Wren's destinations.

### 4.7 Do / Don't
**Do:**
- Show one found-object scale cue per shot.
- Use amber practicals in every night frame.
- Hold 6–12s.
- Give every section a small event.
- Show hands-free craft (tools move, residents build).
- Re-use locked cast text verbatim.
- End each episode with lights on or lights out.

**Don't:**
- Photoreal animals or human faces.
- Text on screen in long-form, except chapter cards.
- Fast cuts, or peril, injury or fire danger. Fire is only a contained stove or candle glow.
- Brand logos (except sponsor props).
- Cold palettes without an amber anchor.
- Characters talking or lip-syncing.
- The same set, same action and same music two uploads in a row.

## 5. Higgsfield production spec

### 5.1 Model routing
| Need | Model | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Default: every story section** | **Seedance 2.5 i2v** `bytedance/seedance-2.5/image-to-video` | `image_url` is the *literal first frame*, so the SOUL keyframe's miniature set and resident are preserved. Optional `end_image_url` locks the final pose. 4–30s, 480p/720p (use **720p**), `generate_audio` defaults to true. |
| Section keyframes (stills) | **SOUL Standard** `higgsfield-ai/soul/standard`, 16:9 or 9:16, 1080p | Locks set, cast and palette before any motion is paid for |
| Establishing wides, weather sweeps, crane-ups over the whole town, "other world" arrivals | **Cinema Studio 4.0** `higgsfield/cinema-studio/4.0` (text/reference-to-video, 4–30s, 720p, `image_urls` refs) | Richer camera language and palette presets. **Use only at hard-cut boundaries**, because it does not take a literal first frame, so it cannot continue a chain. |
| Abstract inserts with no cast (rain on a tin roof, steam) | Seedance 2.5 t2v (16:9 / 9:16) | Cheap, no keyframe needed |

**Cinema Studio 4.0 house settings:**
- `genre: drama` (the most gentle option; never horror or action).
- `pacing: calm`, or `single-shot` for crane-ups.
- `camera_model: 35mm-film`.
- `camera_lens: warm-vintage`, or `halation-vintage` for night.
- `color_palette` by mood:
  - `twilight-fable` for dusk and night.
  - `the-morning-after-rain` for rain and fog mornings.
  - `yellow-room` for interiors.
  - `breakfast-on-schedule` for morning shop openings.
  - `oil-ochre` for autumn.
  - `a-dream-in-color` for spring and summer.
  - `the-faded-fresco` for memory or flashback beats.
  - `pink-velvet` for winter-holiday dusk only.
- Always pass 1–3 SOUL cast/set refs in `image_urls`.

### 5.2 Prompt templates
**Keyframe (SOUL Standard) template:**
```

micro-props, visible craft texture. {CAST BLOCK verbatim}. {ACTION POSE, frozen mid-gesture}.

#F6EAD3, sage #9DB28A, dusk plum #5B4A6B. Weather: {WEATHER}. Season: {SEASON OVERLAY}.
Fine 35mm grain, cozy, warm, no text, no people, no photoreal animals. {16:9 | 9:16}.
```
**Section video (Seedance 2.5 i2v) template:**
```
image_url = {keyframe or previous segment's last frame}

| leaves drifting}. Lighting stays {LIGHT STATE}. Motion continues naturally past the end of
the clip (do not settle to a stop) {omit this line for the final segment of a section}.
Warm cozy palette, fine film grain."

```
**Establishing (Cinema Studio 4.0) template:**
```

image_urls=[set_ref, cast_ref], genre=drama, pacing=calm|single-shot, camera_model=35mm-film,
camera_lens=warm-vintage, color_palette={preset}, duration={4–30}
```

### 5.3 Character consistency
1. **One reference pack per resident:** front, 3/4 and profile SOUL stills, plus one seasonal variant each. It is stored once and re-used. The keyframe prompt always includes the locked cast block verbatim, and the video prompt names the resident plus one action only.
2. **Keyframe-first always.** Never text-to-video a resident. Seedance i2v treats `image_url` as the literal frame 0, so identity is inherited from the approved still.
3. **`end_image_url` pose lock:** generate a second SOUL still for the *intended end pose* (Miso setting the bowl down, Lumen's lamp lit) and pass it as `end_image_url` on the **final segment of a section only**. This does three jobs:
   - It prevents drift at the end of the clip.
   - It guarantees the payoff pose.
   - That end still can be the next section's keyframe, if the edit is a match cut.
4. **Short loops:** set `end_image_url` = the Short's *first* keyframe, so the final frame returns to frame 0 for a seamless loop (§8).
5. **QA gate:** reject any clip where the cast marks drift. The marks are Miso's navy headband and terracotta apron, Bramble's gold spectacles and sage cardigan, Lumen's brass lantern and navy cap, and Wren's terracotta satchel. Re-roll the same seed ±1 before re-keyframing.

### 5.4 Continuity across stitched segments (app rule: ceil(sec/30) gens, each ≥4s)
- **Split evenly:**
  - 45s → 23 + 22.
  - 75s → 25 + 25 + 25.
  - 100s → 25 × 4.
  - 34s → 17 + 17. Never 30 + 4.
- **Within a section the chain is seamless.** The last frame of segment N is the `image_url` of N+1. Therefore:
  - Prompt each segment as **one continuing action** with a *constant* camera move, and repeat the move verb in every segment ("slow lateral dolly left-to-right continues").
  - Do not end a mid-chain segment on a cut, fade or settled pose. Write "motion continues".
  - Keep the light state identical across the chain. Change time of day only at section boundaries.
  - Avoid a resident exiting frame at a boundary, because the next segment would lose them.
- **Between sections, use deliberate hard cuts.** They are allowed and encouraged for time jumps, location changes and Cinema Studio establishing shots. Each section starts from a fresh SOUL keyframe, which resets drift.
- **Remotion:**
  - 6–10 frame audio crossfade at every seam.
  - No video crossfade inside chains (they are already continuous).
  - 12-frame dip-to-warm (`#F2B35E` at 10%) only on deliberate time-jump cuts.

### 5.5 ASMR foley: generate_audio vs. library SFX
| Use Seedance `generate_audio: true` | Use library SFX (generate_audio false or muted) |
|---|---|
| Sync-critical, on-screen foley: a ladle clink, sizzle, knife taps, a door bell, footsteps on wood, rain hitting *that* awning | Signature brand sounds (ident chime, lamp "tink", outro music box), which must be identical every episode |
| One-off textures unique to a scene | Sleep/ambient cuts, which need clean, loop-safe, artifact-free beds |
| Shorts, where speed matters | Any segment where generated audio contains music, voices or mumbling (strip it) |
| — | Room tone and weather beds spanning several sections, laid under the whole episode for seam-free continuity |

**Mix:**
- Foley −18 to −14 LUFS short-term.
- Music bed −26 to −22.
- Narration −16.
- Final master −14 LUFS integrated for YouTube.
- Ducking is automatic in Remotion.

## 6. Intro, outro and branding

### 6.1 Ident (5s): "Lamps On"
Blue-hour wide of Thimble Town (Cinema Studio, `twilight-fable`, `single-shot`).
- **0–1.5s:** Lumen's brass lantern touches the first street lamp, which goes *tink* and glows amber.
- **1.5–4s:** the glow cascades down Lantern Lane and every tiny window lights in sequence, window by window.
- **4–5s:** the thimble water tower catches the last glow, and the wordmark fades in, cream on the plum sky.
- **Audio:** music-box three-note motif (C–E–G, celesta), a soft lamp tink and distant crickets.
- Used on long-form only, after the cold-open hook (at about 0:05–0:10). **Never at 0:00.**

### 6.2 Intro sting (1.5s)
The motif's last note and a single window lighting up. Used as the chapter transition in long-form and optionally at the start of narrated episodes.

### 6.3 Outro (18s) with end screen
- **0–4s:** a slow pull-back from the episode's final lit window to a town wide.
- **4–18s:** the frame holds and the lights dim one by one, leaving space for two end-screen video slots on the left and right and a subscribe circle centred low over the thimble tower.
- **Audio:** a 12s music-box reprise, then rain or crickets to the end.
- A text-free plate is used so that end-screen elements are the only UI.

### 6.4 Logo concepts (SOUL Standard, 1:1 via 16:9 crop)
1. **Primary: "Thimble Tower".** A brass thimble with a tiny arched window and a lit amber lamp inside, on a plum circle. Wordmark in a rounded serif (e.g. Fraunces Soft) in cream.

## 7. Narration and audio style

**Decision: hybrid.**
- The **main track is wordless.** It is universal, has zero localisation cost, and keeps the ASMR audience.
- **A soft-narrated series, "Thimble Town Tales"** (about one episode in four), re-uses the same footage and structure with a storybook narrator. This builds bedtime-story search traffic and gives a second angle on the inauthentic-content risk (§9).

**Narrator persona:** "The Keeper of the Lamps". An unseen, warm, older storyteller. Present tense, simple sensory sentences. Never explains the plot, only notices. Gender-neutral casting is fine. A warm low alto or soft baritone is preferred.
- **Pace:** 105–120 WPM, with 1.5–3s pauses between lines and silence under the payoff shots.
- **ElevenLabs direction:** *"Warm, unhurried bedtime storyteller in their 60s, soft close-mic intimacy, gentle smile in the voice, low energy, no dramatic emphasis, rounded consonants, breathy at phrase ends, like reading to someone already half asleep."* Settings: stability about 0.65, similarity about 0.8, style about 0.15 `[U: tune per voice]`.
- **Sample (40 words):**
  > *The rain came softly to Thimble Town tonight. Miso lifted the shutters, lit the little stove, and set the broth to whisper. One by one, the lanterns answered. Somewhere down the lane, a small snail was already on his way.*

**Music direction:**
- Lo-fi (70–80 BPM, dusty keys, vinyl crackle).
- Music box and celesta motifs (the brand motif C–E–G).
- Fingerpicked nylon acoustic guitar and soft felt piano.
- Seasonal colour:
  - Winter: sleigh-bell whisper and glockenspiel.
  - Autumn: cello pads.
  - Summer: kalimba and cicadas.
  - Spring: flute.
- Music is always under the foley and never tops it. There is no music at all in the first 5s hook.

**ASMR foley list (library plus generated):**
- Kitchen: ladle on ceramic, broth simmer, noodle swish, chopstick taps, knife on tiny board, kettle whistle (far), tea pour, oven door creak, flour puff, rolling pin.
- Bells and devices: shop door bell, paper rustle, envelope seal, pencil scratch, clock tick.
- Weather: rain on tin/awning, drips into a thimble, thunder (distant), wind through leaves, snow crunch.
- Town and nature: footsteps on wood and cobble, lamp tink, lantern squeak, crickets, frogs, fireflies (a soft shimmer), train clack (tiny), boat creak, water lapping.
- Building: wood-block taps, sanding, brush strokes, glue click, tiny hammer.

## 9. Compliance

### 9.1 Inauthentic/repetitive content (YPP)
YouTube's July 15, 2025 update renamed "repetitive content" to **"inauthentic content"**: mass-produced, templated videos with minimal variation, easy to reproduce at scale, and lacking meaningful author input [S23][S24][S25]. **AI ASMR loops are the textbook risk.** Mitigations, which are mandatory:
1. **Story arcs.** Every upload has a beginning, middle and end with a *specific event*: the first customer, a lost letter, a storm or an opening night. The shared structure is a story spine, not a template.
2. **Recurring named characters and canon.** Miso, Bramble, Lumen and Wren, with continuity (seasons, shop menus, Wren's travels). This is authorship YouTube can see.
3. **Seasons plus a calendar.** Episodes happen *in time*, and the world changes (the bakery gets built, the snow melts).
4. **Distinct episodes.** No two consecutive uploads share location plus weather plus activity. The show-bible spreadsheet enforces this, and the app's quality gate should reject a near-duplicate premise `[U: implement check]`.
5. **Sleep cuts are compilations with added value:** a new intro, chapters, a new music bed and a seasonal title card. They are capped at 2 per month and never exceed the count of original episodes.
6. **Visible authorship:** chapter titles, descriptions with "story notes", community posts on canon, and the narrated series.

### 9.2 AI labeling
- YouTube requires the "altered or synthetic" disclosure for **realistic** content a viewer could mistake for a real person, place or event. It does **not** require it for clearly unrealistic, animated or stylised content, or for production assistance [S26][S27]. The test is *whether a reasonable viewer would be misled about reality* [S27].
- **Felt mice running ramen shops are clearly fantastical, so the label is usually not required.**
- **Honest caveat:** our look imitates *real handmade miniatures*. A viewer could believe a real artist built and filmed a real diorama. That is a claim about real-world craft.
- **Channel policy:**
  - The About page and every description say "Stories and worlds created with AI tools".
  - **Toggle the disclosure on** for any video with photoreal human hands ("giant hand" moments), real-world locations rendered realistically (e.g. a tilt-shift version of a real city), or anything framed as "how I built this".
  - On TikTok and Meta, apply the platform AI label by default (their auto-detection via C2PA may apply it anyway `[U]`) [S28].
- Never title anything "I built…" or "handmade". Use "Building a Tiny…", where the residents build.

### 9.3 Made-for-kids designation
- YouTube and the FTC weigh subject matter, intended vs. actual audience, animated characters or toys that appeal to children, kid-directed language, and activities like simple songs or play-acting. It is the creator's legal responsibility to judge the "totality of circumstances" [S29][S30][S31].
- **Position:** Thimble Town is **made for general and adult cozy audiences** (study, sleep, ASMR, cozy-games fans), so **set "No, not made for kids" at channel level, and decide per video.**
- **Guardrails that keep this honest:**
  - No nursery rhymes, counting or ABCs, and no "kids"/"toddlers"/"children" in titles or tags.
  - Narration is written for adults winding down.
  - No toy-unboxing framing.
  - Targeting and thumbnails aim at 18–44.
- **If a video is genuinely aimed at children** (e.g. a future bedtime-story spin-off), mark it MFK. That disables comments, notifications and features like end screens and cards, and it lowers RPM sharply [S29] [S17]. Keep kid-directed content off this channel.
- YouTube may override the designation. Monitor the audience-age analytics monthly.
