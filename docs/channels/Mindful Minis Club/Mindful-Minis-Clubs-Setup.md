# Mindful Minis Club — Channel Concept & Development Guide

**Concept:** Original sing-along videos that help children ages 1–6 solve problems,
regulate emotions, and complete everyday tasks — led by two kid characters and
four animal friends, each of whom owns one calming skill.

**Confirmed direction:**
- **Art:** two tracks tested head-to-head — **Style A** (soft pastel storybook × flat vector hybrid) and **Style B** (watercolor paper-craft).
- **Vocals:** warm adult female lead — the "kind teacher" voice.
- **Production:** storyboard-first (illustrated stills + gentle motion), animate the winners.
- **Cast:** full proposed cast below, with locked reproduction templates.

---

## 1. Positioning

**The promise to caregivers:** *every song teaches one thing a small child can
actually do.* Not entertainment with a lesson bolted on — a tool a parent
reaches for at the exact moment of the meltdown, the toothbrush standoff, the
daycare drop-off.

That promise decides everything downstream:

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| One skill per song, named in the title | Parents search for the *problem* ("calm down song for toddlers"), not for entertainment |
| A repeating chorus that IS the technique | The chorus must be the thing the child does — "smell the flower, blow the candle" — so the song functions outside the video |
| Calm palettes, slow cuts, no flashing | Differentiates from hyper-stimulating competitors; the category's loudest parent complaint |
| Animal per skill | A child who loves Breeze learns breathing; the character *is* the curriculum |

**Competitive frame:** the category is dominated by high-stimulation, high-volume
channels. The white space is *calm, genuinely useful, beautiful* — Super Simple
Songs' utility with Sarah & Duck's temperament. "Mindful" in the name sets that
expectation and pre-qualifies the caregiver.

## 2. The cast

Two children, four animal friends. **Each animal owns exactly one regulation
skill** so episodes are cast-driven and the child can request the skill by
name ("I want Wobble").

### The children

**BO** — boy, age 4. Impulsive, warm, wants to do everything *now*. The one who
feels the problem first. Learns by doing.
**POPPY** — girl, age 5. Thoughtful, a planner, the one who remembers the
technique. Occasionally bossy — which is its own lesson.

Bo and Poppy carry the **task/routine** songs (teeth, tidying, getting dressed)
and the **problem-solving** songs (sharing, taking turns) — because those are
peer problems, not feelings problems.

### The animal friends

| Character | Species | Owns | Personality | Signature move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Breeze** | Bunny | **Breathing / calming down** | Gentle, unhurried, speaks softly | Nose twitch → "smell the flower, blow the candle" |
| **Rumble** | Bear | **Big feelings (anger, frustration)** | Huge, soft-hearted, feels everything loudly | Stomps it out, then goes soft and small |
| **Wobble** | Turtle | **Pausing / patience / a safe space** | Slow, funny, never rushed | Tucks into shell for three counts, comes back ready |
| **Pip** | Bird | **Worry / brave first tries / goodbyes** | Tiny, nervous, brave anyway | Perches on a shoulder; flutters up when brave |

> **Why these four:** they map to the four dominant distress states of ages 1–6 —
> over-arousal (Breeze), anger (Rumble), impulsivity/impatience (Wobble), and
> anxiety/separation (Pip). Wobble's shell-pause is a stylized version of the
> "turtle technique," a genuinely evidence-based preschool self-regulation
> practice — which is worth saying plainly in your channel description.

### Cast expansion rule

Do **not** add a fifth animal until every current one has three songs. Each new
character multiplies the consistency burden and dilutes the child's attachment.
Depth beats roster size in this category.

## 3. Art style — the two-track experiment

Both tracks share the **same cast, same palette logic, same composition rules**.
Only the rendering language differs, so the A/B test isolates style alone.

### Style A — "Soft Pastel Vector"

Flat vector construction (clean shapes, confident outlines, no rendering noise)
warmed by storybook color and the faintest paper grain. Reproduces the most
reliably; animates the most cheaply.

**Locked style string (paste into every image prompt):**
> `Children's picture-book illustration in a soft pastel flat-vector style: clean rounded vector shapes with gentle 3px warm-grey outlines, flat matte fills with subtle soft shading, faintest paper grain, calm muted pastel palette, generous negative space, simple uncluttered background shapes, warm even lighting, no harsh gradients, no photorealism, no text.`

**Palette (use these hex values verbatim):**
`cream #FDF6EC` · `sage #A8C3A0` · `sky #A9D3E8` · `peach #F6C8A8` ·
`coral #F2A099` · `butter #F7DE9B` · `outline warm-grey #5C534A`

### Style B — "Watercolor Paper-Craft"

Cut-paper characters layered over watercolor washes — visible paper fiber,
torn edges, pigment granulation. Slower to lock in, but distinctive and deeply
calming; the strongest bedtime and premium-feel option.

**Locked style string:**
> `Children's picture-book illustration in a cut-paper collage and watercolor style: characters built from torn textured paper with visible fiber edges and subtle drop shadows, layered over soft watercolor washes with natural pigment granulation and bloom, warm muted earth palette, hand-made artisanal feel, generous negative space, soft diffuse light, no digital gloss, no photorealism, no text.`

**Palette:**
`paper #F4EDE0` · `moss #8FA97F` · `dusty-blue #93B4C4` · `terracotta #D89A7A` ·
`blush #E7B7AC` · `ochre #E3C07B` · `ink #4A4239`

### Shared composition rules (both tracks)

- Characters centered or on thirds, **full body or waist-up**, always facing camera or ¾ — never back-of-head as a main frame.
- **Eye level = child's eye level.** Camera never looks down on the characters.
- One clear focal subject per frame; background is shape and color, not detail.
- **No flashing, no strobing, no rapid color changes** (seizure-safety *and* YouTube kids policy).
- 16:9 for main videos, 9:16 for Shorts — generate the Short frames separately rather than cropping.

### The A/B decision protocol

1. Produce **the same song** — "Breathe Like a Bunny" (video #1) — in both styles. Identical lyrics, identical vocal, identical scene list. Style is the only variable.
2. Publish both within 48 hours of each other: one as the main video, one as a Short, then swap the arrangement on song #2 to cancel out format bias.
3. Also produce a third asset per style: a **thumbnail set** (3 candidates each), since style affects CTR more than retention at this age.
4. **Decide after 4 videos (2 per style), not 1.** Judge on, in priority order: (a) average view duration, (b) CTR, (c) which one *you* can look at 200 times.
5. Lock the winner as the channel style. Keep the loser as a documented "special episode" look (e.g., watercolor for bedtime songs) rather than discarding it.

## 4. Character template system (the reproduction engine)

This is the piece that makes a channel instead of a pile of videos. **Every
prompt is assembled from fixed blocks; only the scene block ever changes.**

### The formula

```
[STYLE STRING]  +  [CHARACTER BLOCK(S)]  +  [SCENE/ACTION]  +  [COMPOSITION]  +  [EXCLUSIONS]
```

Nothing else. If a frame comes out wrong, fix the scene block — never quietly
reword a character block, or the character drifts across episodes.

### Locked character blocks

Copy these verbatim. The **identity anchor** (bolded) is the one detail that
must survive every frame — it's what a 3-year-old uses to recognize the
character.

**BO**
> `Bo, a cheerful 4-year-old boy with warm brown skin, short black curly hair, a round face and big round dark eyes, wearing a **mustard-yellow t-shirt with a small white star on the chest**, teal shorts and white sneakers, chunky toddler proportions with a large head and short limbs.`

**POPPY**
> `Poppy, a thoughtful 5-year-old girl with light olive skin, dark brown hair in **two round pigtail puffs tied with coral bobbles**, big round brown eyes, wearing dusty-rose dungarees over a cream long-sleeve top and red canvas shoes, chunky toddler proportions with a large head and short limbs.`

**BREEZE**
> `Breeze, a small gentle bunny with soft lilac-grey fur, long floppy ears with pale pink inner ears, a tiny round tail, calm half-closed eyes, wearing a **mint-green scarf**, rounded simple shapes, about knee-height to the children.`

**RUMBLE**
> `Rumble, a big soft honey-brown bear with a round belly, small rounded ears, gentle sleepy eyes and a tiny nose, wearing a **rust-orange vest**, rounded simple shapes, slightly taller than the children.`

**WOBBLE**
> `Wobble, a slow smiling turtle with sage-green skin and a domed shell patterned with **cream hexagons and a soft butter-yellow rim**, short legs, heavy-lidded calm eyes, rounded simple shapes, about knee-height to the children.`

**PIP**
> `Pip, a tiny round sky-blue bird with a **butter-yellow chest and a single curled tuft feather on the head**, a small orange beak and bright alert eyes, often perched on a shoulder, rounded simple shapes, palm-sized.`

### Standing exclusions (append to every prompt)

> `No text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no watermarks, no extra characters, no scary or sad expressions, no dark or muddy colors, no flashing or high-contrast strobing patterns, no photorealism, no adult characters unless specified.`

### The character-sheet workflow (do this once, before video #1)

1. For **each style track**, generate a **character sheet** per character:
   > `[STYLE STRING] + [CHARACTER BLOCK] + "character reference sheet, the same character shown three times on a plain cream background: front view, three-quarter view, and side view, full body, neutral friendly expression, consistent proportions" + [EXCLUSIONS]`
2. Generate 4–6 candidates. Pick the one that best matches the written block — **not** the prettiest. The written block is the source of truth.
3. Save the winner as the canonical sheet: `mindful-minis/style-a/bo-sheet.png`, etc.
4. Upload the sheets into the project's **reference images** in the app. Every scene generation then carries both the text block *and* the visual reference — text alone drifts; text + reference holds.
5. Also generate one **cast group shot** per style — it becomes your channel art, your thumbnail base plate, and the visual contract for scale relationships between characters.

### Consistency guardrails

- **Never** regenerate a character sheet once locked. If a sheet is wrong, fix it *now*, before episode 1 — after episode 3 it's a rebrand.
- Keep a **STYLE-LOCK.md** in the project with the exact strings, hex codes, and sheet file paths. Paste the whole thing into the project's instructions field so every prompt inherits it automatically.
- Watch for drift on: outfit color, hair volume, eye size, and relative height. Those four break first.
- When a frame needs a new prop or setting, add it to the *scene* block only, e.g. `"...standing at a bathroom sink holding a blue toothbrush, morning light through a round window"`.

## 5. Vocals & music spec

**Voice identity:** warm adult female, mid-range, clear diction, gently bright,
unhurried. Think "favorite preschool teacher" — never breathy, never babyish,
never belting. She sings *with* the child, not *at* them.

**Locked musical style string (use in every song generation):**
> `Gentle children's sing-along, warm friendly adult female lead vocal, clear diction, 92–100 bpm, simple ukulele and soft acoustic guitar, light hand percussion and soft glockenspiel, major key, cheerful and calming, sparse arrangement with plenty of space, no drums-heavy production, no auto-tune, no adult pop styling.`

Bedtime variants: drop to `68–76 bpm`, swap glockenspiel for `soft felt piano and warm pad`, add `lullaby feel, very sparse`.

### Song structure rules (ages 1–6)

- **Chorus lands ≥3 times, word-for-word identical.** Repetition is the product.
- 3–8 words per line. Strong, obvious end rhymes. One idea per song.
- **The chorus contains the actual technique** — the child should be able to perform the skill from the chorus alone.
- Call-and-response and physical actions (breathe, stomp, tuck, clap) every song.
- Target length **90–150 seconds** for skill songs; **~2 minutes exactly** for the toothbrushing song (functional duration = built-in replay).

### ⚠️ The real constraint: vocal consistency across songs

Song models generate their own vocalist per render — **the same style string will
not guarantee the identical singer across episodes** the way a locked image block
guarantees the identical character. Plan for this honestly:

1. **Lock the style string exactly** and never edit it casually — it's the strongest lever you have.
2. **Generate 3 candidates per song** (pennies each) and keep the take closest to your reference vocal. Save an approved "voice reference" song and compare against it every time.
3. **Use one model for all songs.** Switching models switches voice character far more than switching prompts.
4. Accept minor variance. Successful channels in this category *do* vary; the visual cast and the musical arrangement carry brand identity harder than the exact timbre.
5. If drift becomes unacceptable, the fallback is a **spoken-word intro/outro** in a locked ElevenLabs voice bookending each song — that voice *is* perfectly consistent, and it gives the channel a recognizable human host thread.

### Voice test protocol (open task #3)

- Generate the **same two verses + chorus** across all three available models (MiniMax, Lyria, ElevenLabs Music), 3 takes each = 9 candidates, roughly $0.50 total.
- Judge on: warmth, diction clarity at toddler listening distance, singability (can *you* sing along on first hearing), and how it sounds on a **phone speaker** — the actual playback device.
- Pick one model. Save the winning take as the permanent reference. Record the exact style string in STYLE-LOCK.md.
- **Use Lyria when you want timed lyrics** — it returns per-line timings, which gives you exact karaoke/bouncing-word captions. For a sing-along channel that is a genuine feature, not a nicety.

## 6. Production pipeline (storyboard-first)

**One-time setup**
1. Create the project in the app: name `Mindful Minis Club`, niche "children's sing-along songs for emotional regulation, ages 1–6," tone "warm, calm, encouraging."
2. Paste **STYLE-LOCK.md** (style string + palette + all six character blocks + exclusions) into the project's **instructions** field. It now rides into every script and every visual prompt automatically.
3. Upload the character sheets as reference images.

**Per video**
1. `write a song about [topic]` → lyrics, musical style, and one scene per lyric section; the video is laid out with the song as its audio (no narration over it).
2. Review the lyrics *out loud* before spending on visuals. Sing the chorus. If it stumbles, ask for a rewrite — it costs cents and it's the whole product.
3. `continue` → each scene is illustrated in the locked style.
4. Re-roll any weak frame individually ("redo scene 4, wider, more negative space"). Do **not** re-run the whole stage.
5. Render → review on a phone with the sound low, as a parent would.
6. Publish with the **Made for Kids** flag (see §8).

**Cost per storyboard video:** ~$0.035 song + ~$0.02 lyrics + 8–12 stills ≈
**well under $0.50**. That economics is what makes the two-style experiment and
3-candidate vocal selection trivially affordable.

**Upgrade path:** once a song proves itself (watch time, replays), re-generate its
chorus scenes as animated clips and re-render. Only winners earn animation.

## 7. First 10 videos

Ordered for launch: the strongest search intent and clearest utility first.
Each includes a ready-to-paste app prompt.

### Emotional regulation

**1. "Breathe Like a Bunny"** — *Breeze* · calming down · 100s
**Chorus:** *"Smell the flower — blow the candle — Breathe like a bunny, we can handle."*
The flagship. Highest parent search intent in the entire category, and the technique is genuinely portable.
> `write a song about breathing slowly to calm down, led by Breeze the bunny, using "smell the flower, blow the candle"`

**2. "Rumble's Big Feelings"** — *Rumble* · anger & frustration · 110s
**Chorus:** *"Big, big feelings — stomp them out. I can be mad and not shout."*
Validates the feeling, redirects the behavior. The distinction most kids' songs miss.
> `write a song about what to do with big angry feelings, led by Rumble the bear, stomping the feeling out safely`

**3. "The Turtle Pause"** — *Wobble* · stop and think · 95s
**Chorus:** *"Tuck in, one-two-three — pop back out, the calm is me."*
The turtle technique, made singable. Best "before you react" tool on the channel.
> `write a song about pausing and counting to three before reacting, led by Wobble the turtle tucking into his shell`

**4. "Not Yet, But Soon"** — *Pip* · frustration with hard things · 100s
**Chorus:** *"I can't do it — not yet, not yet! I try again and I'm not upset."*
Growth mindset for preschoolers. Enormous caregiver appeal.
> `write a song about trying something hard and saying "not yet" instead of "I can't", led by Pip the bird learning to fly`

**5. "Bye-Bye Bravely"** — *Pip + Poppy* · separation / drop-off · 105s
**Chorus:** *"Wave, wave, brave — I'll see you soon. You keep the morning, I'll keep the afternoon."*
Drop-off anxiety is a daily, searched-for, emotionally urgent problem.
> `write a song about saying goodbye bravely at daycare drop-off and knowing the grown-up comes back, led by Pip and Poppy`

### Tasks & routines

**6. "Toothy Time"** — *Bo + Poppy* · brushing teeth · **exactly 2:00**
**Chorus:** *"Round and round and round we go — top and bottom, high and low!"*
Functional duration = the song IS the timer. Parents replay it twice daily, forever.
> `write a song about brushing teeth for two minutes, covering top, bottom, front and back, led by Bo and Poppy`

**7. "Tidy-Up Train"** — *whole cast* · cleaning up · 90s
**Chorus:** *"Chugga-chugga, pick it up — one more toy and the train's not stuck!"*
Call-and-response, whole-cast energy, immediately actionable.
> `write a song about tidying up toys together like a train collecting cars, led by the whole cast`

**8. "Sleepy Sun, Sleepy Me"** — *Breeze + Wobble* · bedtime wind-down · **150s, 70 bpm**
**Chorus:** *"Sleepy sun is going down — sleepy me in my pajama gown."*
Bedtime is the category's watch-time engine and the anchor for your first compilation. **Produce this one in Style B (watercolor).**
> `write a gentle slow bedtime song about the sun going down and getting sleepy, led by Breeze and Wobble, lullaby feel`

### Problem solving

**9. "Two Turns"** — *Bo + Poppy* · sharing & taking turns · 100s
**Chorus:** *"Your turn, my turn — two turns, we both learn."*
The single most common preschool conflict, resolved in four words.
> `write a song about taking turns with a favorite toy, led by Bo and Poppy learning "your turn, my turn"`

**10. "Wait, Wait, Wiggle"** — *Wobble + Bo* · patience & waiting · 95s
**Chorus:** *"Wait, wait, wiggle your toes — waiting's easy when the wiggle goes."*
Gives waiting a physical outlet instead of demanding stillness — which is what actually works at this age.
> `write a song about waiting patiently by wiggling your fingers and toes while you wait, led by Wobble and Bo`

### Publishing sequence

Launch **1, 6, 2** first (calm-down, teeth, anger) — highest search intent and
most distinct from each other. Then 3, 9, 5. Then the rest. At video 8, cut a
**"Calm Down Songs" compilation** (all four regulation songs, 20+ min) — in
this category compilations out-earn singles on watch time and become the
channel's most durable asset.

## 8. Channel setup, COPPA & Made for Kids (open task #2)

### YouTube — order of operations

1. **Create a Brand Account channel** (not a personal one) — it can be transferred and co-managed later. Handle: reserve `@MindfulMinisClub` plus the Instagram match.
2. **Set the channel-level kids designation:** YouTube Studio → Settings → Channel → Advanced settings → *"Yes, set this channel as made for kids."* This makes every upload default to Made for Kids.
3. **Confirm per-video** on each upload — the video-level setting is the one that legally binds, even with the channel default set.
4. **Channel description** should state plainly: original songs, ages 1–6, calm pacing, no ads-like content, techniques used. Caregivers read this.
5. **Do not enable** Shorts remixing of your audio if you want to control the brand; decide deliberately.

### What "Made for Kids" actually costs you

Accept these before launch — they reshape strategy:

| Disabled | Consequence for you |
|---|---|
| Personalized ads | **Substantially lower RPM** than general content. Volume + watch time + compilations are the revenue path, not per-view rate |
| Comments | No community feedback loop — your only signal is retention data |
| End screens, cards, watermarks | Discovery must come from thumbnail, title, and **playlists** |
| Notification bell, Stories, Community posts | No push channel to your audience — the algorithm is your only distribution |
| Save-to-playlist by viewers, Super Chat, memberships | No direct monetization from fans |

**Strategic consequence:** build **playlists** obsessively (they're your end
screen), title for *parent search intent*, and treat compilations as primary
products rather than afterthoughts.

### COPPA compliance essentials

- **Collect nothing from children.** No sign-ups, no forms, no email capture aimed at kids, ever — on any surface.
- The Made for Kids designation handles YouTube-side data limits (no personalized ads, restricted data collection).
- **Mis-designating is the real risk** — FTC actions in this category have been expensive. When in doubt, designate as Made for Kids.
- Keep **no** analytics that could identify a child. Your app stores only video-level aggregates — keep it that way.
- If you ever add a website: no tracking pixels on kid-facing pages, and a plain-language privacy policy.

### Instagram — a parent-facing account, not a kid-facing one

Meta requires users to be **13+**, so Instagram cannot be a children's audience.
Position it explicitly for **caregivers**: the technique behind each song, calm-
parenting tips, character art, behind-the-scenes. Bio should read as a parent
resource. This is both compliant and better marketing — the parent is the one
who presses play.

### YouTube API keys (for the app's publishing path)

1. Google Cloud Console → new project (`mindful-minis`).
2. **Enable "YouTube Data API v3."**
3. OAuth consent screen → External → add yourself as a test user → scopes: `youtube.upload` and `youtube.readonly`.
4. Credentials → Create OAuth client ID → **Web application** → add your redirect URI.
5. Complete the OAuth flow once to obtain a **refresh token**; paste the client ID, secret, and refresh token into the project's settings in the app (per-project channel connection is already supported).
6. Optionally create a separate **API key** for read-only analytics/research calls.

> ⚠️ **Engineering to-do before the first automated upload:** the app's publish
> path must send `status.selfDeclaredMadeForKids = true` on every upload for this
> project. Without it, uploads land un-designated and must be fixed by hand — a
> compliance risk, not just an inconvenience. This is a small, well-scoped change
> to the publish adapter; flag it and I'll implement it.

## 9. Critical notes & risks

- **Quality bar is a policy issue here, not just taste.** YouTube actively
  demotes low-effort children's content. Every video must be genuinely original,
  calm, and useful — which is exactly the strategy anyway.
- **No flashing, strobing, or rapid cuts.** Seizure safety, platform policy, and
  the whole premise of the channel.
- **Nothing frightening — ever.** No peril, no chase, no sad endings, no
  characters in distress beyond a feeling that resolves within the song.
- **Everything original.** No existing melodies, no trademarked characters, no
  real children's voices or likenesses. Generating your own music is what makes
  this cleanly ownable — protect that.
- **No real children appear or are recorded.** Full stop.
- **Captions on every video.** For a sing-along, captions are the product
  (karaoke), plus accessibility and watch-time gains. Use the timed-lyric model
  where you can.
- **Thumbnails:** one character, one clear emotion, ≤3 words, huge type. Test
  face-forward vs. action poses early.
- **Titles for the parent, not the child:** *"Calm Down Song for Toddlers 🐰
  Breathe Like a Bunny | Mindful Minis Club"* — problem first, brand last.
- **Pace yourself against the compilation:** once you have 8–10 songs you have a
  20–40 minute asset that will likely out-perform everything else on the channel.
- **Retention truth:** for ages 1–6, *replays* matter more than completion.
  Watch for songs that get re-watched, and make more of those.

### The first session, concretely

1. Create the project; paste STYLE-LOCK.md into instructions.
2. Generate the six character sheets in **Style A**; pick and save.
3. Repeat in **Style B**.
4. Run the voice test; lock the model and style string.
5. `write a song about breathing slowly to calm down, led by Breeze the bunny, using "smell the flower, blow the candle"`.
6. Read the lyrics aloud. Sing the chorus. Only then spend on visuals.
7. Produce it twice — once per style. Compare on a phone, at low volume, as a tired parent.
