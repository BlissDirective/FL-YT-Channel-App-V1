## 1. Niche breakdown and 2026 market trends

### 1.1 What the niche actually is

Three content lanes share one audience:

| Lane | What it covers | Share of output | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Real megaprojects** (news-anchored) | Status, cost, engineering of projects being built or stalled now | ~45% | Search demand spikes with every news cycle; high trust; sponsor-safe |
| **Built or Busted** (reality check) | Projects that shrank, paused or failed | ~20% | The 2026 megaproject news is mostly bad news, and "what went wrong" outperforms hype [U] |
| **City of 2075 / Impossible Engineering** (speculative, grounded) | Space elevators, arcologies, 2075 street life, based on real research | ~35% | Evergreen, and it's where AI visuals beat stock footage. No competitor can film it |

The edge is that **AI visualization is the only way to film the unbuilt**. The B1M and
Megaprojects rely on developer renders and stock footage. We can show a consistent,
cinematic version of any project from any angle, as long as it is **labelled as a
render** (see §9).

### 1.2 Demand drivers in 2026 (verified where cited)

1. **The Gulf giga-project reset is the biggest story in the niche.**
   - NEOM halted work on The Line until after 2030. The sovereign fund is shifting
     money to ports, data centres and Oxagon (about $3B still allocated). Trojena
     and the Red Sea tourism destinations were also paused. The NEOM 2030 population
     target fell from 1.5M to 300K to 100K.
     ([Semafor, 2026-05-22](https://www.semafor.com/article/05/22/2026/saudis-neom-halts-work-on-the-line-until-after-2030);
     [Euronews, 2026-01-26](https://www.euronews.com/2026/01/26/neom-no-more-saudi-arabia-reduces-ambitious-plans-for-the-line-and-futuristic-megacity);
     [Arch Paper, 2026-06](https://www.archpaper.com/2026/06/neom-temporary-work-pause-the-line/))
   - The Line's first phase was reportedly cut to about 2.4 km for about 300K people.
     A leaked audit put the full cost at about $8.8T with completion around 2080 [U,
     reported via WSJ in secondary sources].
   - Riyadh's **Mukaab**: excavation was about 86% complete when above-ground work
     was suspended in January 2026. The target moved from 2030 to about 2040
     ([Wikipedia: Mukaab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukaab); [Riyadh2030.ai](https://www.riyadh2030.ai/giga-projects/new-murabba-update/) [U: secondary]).
   - The counter-story is **Jeddah Tower**. It reached 113 floors and about 454 m by
     2026-09-02, climbing about one floor a week, with an August 2028 completion target
     ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/jeddah-tower-worlds-tallest-skyscraper-construction-milestone-12401838); [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/real-estate/jeddah-tower-tallest-by-2028)).
2. **Capital cities from scratch are shrinking or pivoting.**
   - Indonesia's **Nusantara** 2026 budget is Rp6.26T (about $387M), less than half
     of 2025 and about 85% below 2024. It has been re-scoped as a "political capital"
     by 2028 ([Indonesia Business Post](https://indonesiabusinesspost.com/5017/jakarta-power-play/indonesia-allocates-rp6-26-trillion-for-ikn-in-2026-down-from-previous-years); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/12/12/nusantara-political-capital-construction-indonesia/); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/16/g-s1-117409/indonesia-capital-nusantara-future-faces-doubts-present)).
   - Egypt's **New Administrative Capital** has the Iconic Tower (394 m, Africa's
     tallest). The monorail's East Nile Line (53 km, about 60 min) opened to
     passengers on 2026-05-06 ([Wikipedia: Iconic Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iconic_Tower_(Egypt)); [Wikipedia: Cairo Monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Monorail)).
   - China's **Xiong'an** has about 1.41M residents and 5,300+ buildings on about
     215 km², against a 5M target ([Wikipedia: Xiong'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiong'an); [gov.cn 2026-03](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202603/28/content_WS69c73f01c6d00ca5f9a0a252.html)).
3. **Climate adaptation is the most durable driver.**
   - **Floating cities.** OCEANIX Busan (UN-Habitat + BIG) slipped from a 2023 start
     to a 2025 start, with the first module by about 2028. Its 2026 on-water status is
     [U] ([UN-Habitat](https://unhabitat.org/news/27-apr-2022/un-habitat-and-partners-unveil-oceanix-busan-the-worlds-first-prototype-floating); [Oceanix](https://oceanix.com/busan/)).
     The **Maldives Floating City** (Dutch Docklands) plans about 20K residents and
     brain-coral hexagons. It has repeatedly slipped and now targets about 2027
     ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_Floating_City)).
   - **Land-building.** Copenhagen's **Lynetteholm** is a storm-surge peninsula that
     faces lawsuits ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/style/article/lynetteholm-controversy-copenhagen)).
     Lagos's **Eko Atlantic** is about 80% reclaimed, with a target of 300K residents
     ([Nigeria Housing Market guide](https://www.nigeriahousingmarket.com/guides/eko-atlantic-city-guide) [U: secondary]).
4. **Transport moonshots.**
   - **Hyperloop** has no passenger line anywhere in 2026. Hardt reached 85 km/h
     with a lane switch on the 420 m European Hyperloop Center track in Veendam
     ([Hardt](https://www.hardt.global/press/hardt-hyperloop-sets-speed-record-and-demonstrates-lane-switching-at-european-hyperloop-center)).
     China's T-Flight has reportedly run at about 623 km/h (387 mph) in a
     low-vacuum tube ([Electronic Specifier](https://www.electronicspecifier.com/products/design-automation/china-s-t-flight-hyperloop-train-sets-new-speed-record/)).
   - **Dubai Loop** (Boring Company + RTA): a 6.4 km pilot with 4 stations and

   - **Toyota Woven City** launched on 2025-09-25. Phase 1 holds about 300–360

   - **Hong Kong Northern Metropolis**: about 2.5M residents and 650K jobs. The

6. **Urbanism debates.**
   - **The 15-minute city** is still polarizing. In Paris, Hidalgo's successor
     Emmanuel Grégoire won on 2026-03-22 with 50.52%, a mandate for continuity

     Plenty filed for bankruptcy (Mar 2025), AeroFarms shut its Virginia farm

   - **Huajiang Grand Canyon Bridge** opened on 2025-09-28 and was certified in

**Takeaway:** 2026 is the year of the **reality check**. Audiences have seen the hype
renders. What they now search for is *"is it still happening?"*, *"what actually got
built?"*, and *"what went wrong?"* That favours a channel whose brand promise is
**"the render vs. the reality"**.

### 2.1 Best-performing video styles (patterns [U], synthesized from the channels above)

1. **Status check on a famous project** ("What happened to The Line?"). It uses news
   momentum and prior awareness.
2. **Scale comparison** ("X is so big that…"). It is visual-first and loops well in
   Shorts.
3. **Failure autopsy** ("Why X failed"). It creates tension and suits a documentary
   arc.
4. **Speculative walk-through** ("A day in 2075"). It is evergreen, and only possible
   with AI or animation.
5. **Engineering "how is this even possible"** (space elevator, 1 km tower). It has
   high educational RPM.

### 2.2 Hook structure (first 30 seconds)

| Time | Beat | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 s | **Visual paradox + one-line claim** | "This is the most expensive city on Earth. Almost none of it exists." |
| 5–15 s | **Stakes/number** | "$500 billion. 170 kilometres. Then, in May 2026, the cranes stopped." |
| 15–30 s | **Promise + roadmap** | "Here's what was built, what was cut, and what the render never showed you." |

No logo before 0:05. The 4–6 s ident plays *after* the hook line (§6.1).

### 2.3 Retention devices

- **Render vs Reality split-screen** every 90–120 s: the developer vision against the
  labelled status today.
- **Scale anchors**: an Empire State Building, Eiffel Tower or football-pitch
  silhouette appears in the data HUD.
- **Open loops**: "We'll come back to the problem nobody mentions: water."
- **Status stamps**: a Remotion overlay stamp reading BUILT / BUILDING / PAUSED /
  CANCELLED / CONCEPT on each project.
- **Number countdowns** in the HUD (cost ticker, floors counter, km counter).
- **Pattern interrupt** every 45–60 s: a camera-grammar change (aerial → human
  eye-level), a music drop, or a hard cut.

### 2.4 Chaptering

- Use 5–9 chapters with timestamps in the description; the first chapter must start at
  0:00.
- Name chapters as questions or claims ("Why it paused", "The water problem"), not
  labels ("Part 2").
- Put the last chapter on the verdict ("Built or Busted?"). It drives comments.

### 2.5 Length sweet spots

- **Long-form:** 8–12 min is the core. News status checks run 6–9 min and flagship
  deep dives 12–15 min. At 8 min or more you can place mid-rolls.
- **Shorts:** 45–75 s for scale and reveal pieces, 90–180 s for mini-explainers (Shorts
  up to 3 min are allowed [U: check the current limit]). TikTok requires 60 s or more
  for Creator Rewards eligibility [U: per the parent research doc].

## 4. Unified style bible

### 4.1 Visual language

- **Look:** *architectural-visualization cinematography*. Clean geometry, physically
  plausible materials (glass, weathered concrete, CLT timber, photovoltaic skin),
  volumetric atmosphere, and real-world scale cues (people, trees, cars).
- **Drone reveals** are the channel's signature move. Start low or tight on a detail,
  then rise and pull back to reveal the whole megastructure.
- **Before/after timelines:** the same locked camera position with a time jump
  (desert → foundations → finished). Split across stitched segments so each state
  reads on its own.
- **Blueprint → photoreal** is the brand device (see §4.4).
- **Reality grounding:** real-project episodes pair each render with a status stamp
  and an "AI VISUALIZATION" label (see §9).
- **Tone:** awe with restraint. Beautiful, but never propaganda. The camera admires;
  the narration questions.

### 4.4 Recurring on-screen devices (built in Remotion unless noted)

1. **Blueprint → Photoreal.** Generate two clips with the same camera move. Clip A is
   a navy blueprint wireframe with cyan lines; clip B is photoreal. Remotion wipes
   between them with a 12-frame scan line in cyan. Use it in the cold open and at
   every "reveal" beat. **Prefer Seedance image-to-video** for clip B, with
   `image_url` = the last blueprint frame restyled via image generation, so geometry
   matches [U: test].
2. **Data HUD.** A lower-left monospace panel: project name, location, status stamp,
   and a live counter (cost / height / km / population). It has a thin cyan frame and
   corner ticks, and slides in over 8 frames.
3. **Status Stamp.** A rubber-stamp animation (BUILT / BUILDING / PAUSED / CANCELLED
   / CONCEPT) in the status colours. It is the Built or Busted verdict device.
4. **Scale Ghost.** A translucent cyan silhouette of a known landmark (Empire State
   381 m roof, Eiffel 330 m, Burj Khalifa 828 m, football pitch 105 m) composited
   beside the structure.
5. **Timeline Scrubber.** A horizontal year bar (2021 → 2030 → 2075) with a playhead.
   It is used for before/after jumps.
6. **Render vs Reality split.** Left: "THE PITCH" (render). Right: "STATUS 2026"
   (stamp plus facts on navy). Never use fabricated "real" footage (§9).
7. **Map Pin Fly-in.** Stylized globe → region → site, in a navy/cyan vector style
   (Remotion SVG, not AI).

### 4.5 Camera grammar

| Beat type | Shot size | Lens feel | Default movement | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Establishing** | Extreme wide / aerial | 24 mm equivalent | Drone rise + slow push, or orbital arc 30–60° | Horizon in the top third; structure fills 60% or more of the width by the end |
| **Scale comparison** | Wide, locked or slow lateral | 35 mm | Lateral truck parallel to the structure; human or vehicle in the foreground | Always include a known-size object |
| **Detail / engineering** | Medium to close-up | 50–85 mm | Slow push-in, or rack focus across the joint/module | Macro on materials, bolts, modules |
| **Human scale** | Eye-level medium wide | 35 mm | Gimbal walk-forward at walking speed | Figures from behind or at distance; **no identifiable faces** |
| **Reveal** | Tight → extreme wide | 24 mm | Push through the obstacle (cloud, gate, tunnel mouth) then crane up | Save for once per section at most |
| **Timelapse / before-after** | Wide, locked | 35 mm | Locked tripod; only time changes | Same prompt camera text in each segment |
| **Tension / Busted** | Medium, low angle | 35 mm | Slow dolly-back, empty spaces, stalled cranes | Desaturate one step (see palettes) |

**Rules:**

- Move one axis at a time.
- Let a move breathe for at least 4 s.
- No handheld shake, except in "construction site" beats.
- Speed: slow (establishing), medium (transport), static (data).

### 4.6 Lighting and time-of-day rules

- **Default: golden hour → blue hour.** Warm key, cool fill, lit windows at blue hour.
- **Engineering detail: overcast noon.** Soft, even light, true materials.
- **Busted / stalled: flat midday haze or fog.** Low saturation, dust.
- **2075 speculative: morning after rain.** Wet reflective streets and clean air
  signal optimism without neon cliché.
- **Night is rare.** Use it only for transport and data-flow episodes.
- Keep time of day **constant within a section** (continuity; see §5.5).

**Do:**

- Show real scale cues.
- Show weather and atmosphere.
- Use plausible materials, construction cranes on active projects, and green
  infrastructure where the plan includes it.
- Show real geography (desert, coast, mountains) consistent with the actual site.

**Don't:**

- Use sci-fi clichés (flying cars everywhere, hologram ads, cyberpunk neon) unless the
  script is specifically about them.
- Show identifiable faces, real people, or real logos and brands.
- Present an invented skyline as the real city today.
- Use Dutch angles, whip pans, or lens flares on every shot.
- Use text inside the AI generation (all text is added in Remotion).

### 5.3 Section prompt template

```

[SCALE] {scale_cue: tiny human figures walking / cars on a highway / a cargo ship} for scale.
[ARCH] {materials}, {form language}, {key feature}.

[MOTION] Camera {movement} at {slow|medium} constant speed, one continuous move, no cuts.
[CONTINUITY] {only for chained segments: "continues seamlessly from previous shot; same camera height, heading, speed, lighting"}.

```

**Worked example (The Line establishing, 30 s):**

```

render state. Tiny road and trucks at its base for scale. Mirrored glass facade
reflecting the mountains, sharp continuous roofline. Golden hour, light dust haze,
sun low behind camera. Camera rises and pushes forward at slow constant speed, one
continuous move, no cuts. Architectural visualization, photoreal, cinematic.
```

With reference images: `"...matching the massing of <<<image_1>>> and the facade

generated stills, or CC-licensed/press images where terms allow; see §9).

### 5.4 Negative / avoid guidance

Cinema Studio has no negative-prompt field [U], so express avoidance positively in the
prompt and enforce it in the QA gate.

- Append to every prompt: *"no on-screen text, no logos, no watermarks, no
  identifiable faces, no cuts, no morphing buildings"*.
- **Reject clips with any of these:**
  - geometry that melts or morphs mid-shot
  - buildings that change floor count
  - water flowing uphill
  - more than one sun or conflicting shadows
  - garbled text or signage
  - people with distorted limbs in the foreground
  - brand logos
  - a recognizable real person
  - camera jitter at the segment boundary
- Avoid prompting the names of famous architects ("in the style of Zaha Hadid") and
  real brand names.
- For real projects, describe geometry instead of relying on the name. The model may
  hallucinate a wrong building.

### 5.5 Continuity rules across stitched segments

The app splits any section over 30 s into `ceil(sec/30)` generations. Each is at least
4 s, and the last frame of segment N becomes the keyframe of N+1.

**Split convention for this channel:** greedy 30 s chunks with the remainder last
(45 → 30+15, 75 → 30+30+15). Section lengths are chosen so the remainder is never
1–3 s. If a script edit creates one (e.g. 62 s), rebalance (62 → 30+28+4) or trim
the section to 60 s.

1. **One continuous move per section.** Write the camera move for the whole section
   (e.g. "slow orbital arc left around the tower"). Each segment prompt repeats the
   *same* move, direction, speed and height band. Nothing starts or stops at a
   boundary.
2. **Self-contained action per segment.** Every segment must also work alone: its
   own subject in frame, no action that starts in N and resolves in N+1 (such as a
   door opening or a train entering and exiting).
3. **Locked variables per section:** time of day, weather, palette, lens, camera
   model, era, and subject materials. Put them in a `section_lock` string that is
   prepended to each segment prompt.
4. **Boundary hygiene:** avoid fast motion, particles or explosions, and near-camera
   objects in the last second of a segment. Keyframes with motion blur degrade the
   next segment.
5. **Progressive framing:** for long drone moves, each segment prompt states where
   the camera *starts* ("continuing from a mid-altitude position facing north") so
   the handoff is coherent.
6. **Hard cuts only at section boundaries**, on narration beats (new idea, number
   reveal, verdict). Transitions between sections are Remotion-controlled: a hard
   cut, a blueprint wipe, or a 6–8 frame dip to navy.
7. **Series continuity:** store approved hero stills per project (e.g.
   `the-line_hero_dusk.png`) and pass them as `image_urls` refs in later episodes so
   the same project looks the same across the channel.

### 5.6 Cost controls

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Draft at 480p, final at 720p | Only the approved script gets the 720p pass |

## 7. Narration voice style

- **Persona: "The Surveyor."** An unseen, well-travelled engineer-journalist. Curious,
  sceptical, fair. Admires ambition, respects physics and budgets. Never mocks the
  people involved, only scrutinizes the plans.
- **Pacing:** **145–155 WPM** baseline. Slow to about 130 WPM on reveals and verdicts;
  speed up to about 165 WPM on number runs. Leave 0.6–1.0 s pauses after big numbers.
- **Tone:** awe with restraint, dry wit (about one wry line per 90 s), no hype
  adjectives ("insane", "mind-blowing" are banned).
- **Accent:** neutral transatlantic / soft British RP. It reads as documentary
  authority to US and UK audiences.
- **ElevenLabs voice direction:**
  > "Warm, resonant baritone documentary narrator in his 40s, neutral transatlantic
  > accent. Measured and clear, with quiet awe held in restraint; slight smile in the
  > voice on wry lines; crisp consonants on numbers; never breathy, never salesy."
  - Settings starting point [U, tune by ear]:
    - stability 0.45–0.55
    - similarity 0.75
    - style 0.15–0.25
    - speaker boost on
  - Use a library or designed voice, **never a clone of a real person**.
- **Sample paragraph (40 words):**
  > "From the ground it looks like a mirror left in the desert. From the air, it looks
  > like a promise: one hundred and seventy kilometres of city. But in May 2026, the
  > cranes went quiet. So what, exactly, got built?"

## 9. Compliance

1. **YouTube AI disclosure.** Set **"Altered or synthetic content" = Yes** for any
   video with photoreal renders of **real places** (Riyadh, Jeddah, Busan, Maldives,
   Copenhagen, etc.) or realistic depictions of real projects. YouTube requires
   disclosure for realistic content a viewer could mistake for a real place or event,
   including *"altering a real cityscape to make it appear different than in reality"*
   ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/)).
   Fully speculative City of 2075 content is arguably "clearly unrealistic", but
   **disclose anyway** (channel policy: disclose all photoreal).
   - TikTok: AIGC label on.
   - Meta: "AI info" label on.
2. **Renders vs. real sites.**
   - Every real-project render carries a persistent small corner tag: **"AI
     VISUALIZATION · NOT ACTUAL FOOTAGE"** (Inter 500, 22 px, 70% white).
   - Status beats use the Status Stamp plus a dated source line ("Source: Semafor,
     22 May 2026").
   - Never show a render captioned as "today", "live" or "drone footage of".
   - When showing "what exists now", use a stylized map/diagram or a clearly labelled
     render ("Approximation of the site, based on reported status").
   - If real photos or video of a site are ever used, they must be licensed or CC,
     credited on screen, and never mixed without a label.
3. **No real-person likeness.**
   - No generated faces of real people (MBS, Musk, Prabowo, architects, etc.).
   - Refer to them by name in narration only, with neutral, sourced statements. Use
     the stylized silhouette / name card device if needed.
   - Crowds and figures are generic, distant, or seen from behind.
   - No voice cloning.
4. **Fact-checking real projects (the review gate is mandatory before render):**
   - For every project claim, log the **status, cost, dates, height/length and
     population** with a URL and access date in the script's source table.
   - Prefer primary sources (developer, government, UN-Habitat) or tier-1 press. Cite
     "reported" figures as reported ("a leaked audit reportedly put…").
   - Put a **"Figures as of [date]"** card in each real-project video, and pin a
     corrections comment when facts change.
   - Anything unconfirmed is flagged [U] in the script, then either softened in
     narration ("reportedly", "planned") or cut.
   - Re-verify NEOM, Mukaab, Nusantara, Busan and Maldives statuses within **7 days**
     of publishing. They change fast.
5. **Trademarks and IP.**
   - Use project names editorially (nominative use).
   - Don't copy developer renders into `image_urls` unless licensed. Describe the
     geometry instead.
   - No developer or brand logos on screen.
6. **Inauthentic content (YouTube, July 2025 and later).**
   - Each episode has a distinct thesis, script and shot plan.
   - The series format is explicitly allowed when each episode has its own storyline.
   - Log the human review of hook, script, fact-check and final cut in the app's
     review gates.
   - Vary the structure between episodes (the 6 series have different arcs, §2.1).
7. **Sponsorships:** tick "includes paid promotion". Sponsors never influence Built or
   Busted verdicts.
