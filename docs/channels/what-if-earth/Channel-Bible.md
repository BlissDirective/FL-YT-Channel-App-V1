# What If Earth: Channel Bible

A faceless, fully AI-produced channel of planet-scale speculative simulations.
Examples: the Moon disappears, Earth stops spinning, the Sahara turns green, or
all the ice melts. It also covers *Earth in 1 Million Years* and *Alternate Earths*.

- **Working name:** What If Earth. The recommended brand is **Earthlines** (see §3).
- **Audience:** 12–40, broad and evergreen.
- **Stack:** Higgsfield Cinema Studio 4.0 as the default, Seedance 2.5 for keyframe-exact shots, ElevenLabs for narration and Remotion for assembly, captions and the HUD.
- **Date:** 2026-09-27.

> **Conventions.** `[U]` marks a claim that is unverified or is an estimate that needs a live check before you rely on it. Source URLs are inline and collected in §10. Subscriber counts are approximate and were taken from third-party trackers on the dates noted.

## 1. Niche breakdown and 2026 market trends

### 1.1 What the niche is
"Speculative science" takes a single counterfactual premise and follows it through
real physics, climate and biology on an escalating timeline. The format is proven at scale:
- Underknown's **What If** has about **8.9M subscribers and 1.7B views across about 1.6K videos**, per Social Blade (https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/whatifscienceshow). CreatorDB puts it at about 11.8M followers across platforms as of June 2026 (https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/what-if/).
- Melodysheep's *Timelapse of the Future* passed **110M views** by November 2025 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timelapse_of_the_Future).

**Why it works:**
- **Curiosity-gap titles.** A question title opens a loop that only the video closes.
- **Built-in escalation.** "What happens next?" gives natural retention hooks at every timeline jump.
- **Evergreen.** "What if the Moon disappeared" gets searched every year, and the physics doesn't expire.
- **Visual spectacle.** Planet-scale disaster is exactly what AI video models now render convincingly.

### 2.1 Best video styles and details (what top performers share)

**Hooks (0–5s)** use one of three patterns:
1. **The premise stated as happening *now*.** "At 9:41 tonight, the Moon vanishes."
2. **The counterintuitive consequence first.** "If the Moon disappeared, the first thing you'd notice isn't the dark. It's the tides."
3. **The scale anchor.** "Seventy meters. That's how far the sea rises if every glacier melts."

Always show the most spectacular frame in seconds 0–3. Never open on a logo.

**Escalation structure.** The spine of every episode is a timeline ladder:
1. **T−0 (normal baseline).** 5–10s, only to set the "before."
2. **T+1 second / minute.** The immediate physics.
3. **T+1 hour / day.** The first human-scale effects.
4. **T+1 week / year.** The system responses: weather, oceans, crops.
5. **T+100 / 10,000 years.** The biosphere adapts.
6. **T+1 million years.** The new equilibrium.
7. **Twist or reflection.** "The strange part is, this already happened once…" Then the CTA and the next question.

Each rung opens with the **T+ clock slam** (the HUD in §4.3), which gives a re-hook about every 45–75s.

**Length sweet spots:**
- **Long-form:** 8–12 min. That clears the mid-roll threshold, matches What If and Unveiled, and costs about $100–$150 in video at $0.2057/s.
- **Deep-future epic:** 12–15 min, at most one a month.
- **Shorts:** 45–75s for virality and 2–4 min for "mini-sims" on YouTube and TikTok.

## 3. Name assessment

"What If" is a crowded mark space:
- Underknown's **What If**: 8.9M subscribers on YouTube, plus a "What If Kids" spin-off.
- Marvel/Disney **"MARVEL WHAT IF…"** registrations: goods classes 9 and 28 found, plus "MARVEL STUDIOS WHAT IF…?" filings in 2022 (https://trademarks.justia.com/885/24/marvel-what-88524736.html).
- xkcd's ***What If?*** books.

"What If" alone is descriptive and weak as a mark. The real risk is **consumer confusion with Underknown's What If channel in class 41, education and entertainment** [U; not legal advice, so run a USPTO/EUIPO knockout search and ideally get counsel]. It is also a search problem: our channel would be buried under @WhatIfScienceShow.

**Before locking the name:**

## 4. Unified style bible

### 4.1 Look
- **Photoreal planetary cinematography.** NASA "Blue Marble" and ISS-window realism: thin blue atmospheric limb, real cloud texture, specular sun glint on oceans, city lights on the night side.
- **Orbital views.** Stable, slow and weighty; the planet never "wobbles." Earth rotates west→east, so with north up, surface features drift **left→right**. Keep that consistent across every segment.
- **IMAX landscape scale.** Ground shots always include a scale anchor: a lone figure, a highway, a wind turbine or a lighthouse. Low horizon and wide lenses.
- **The timeline-counter HUD.** A Remotion overlay, never generated in video. See §4.3.
- **The "changed Earth" signature.** Each episode has one hero color deviation from the Blue Marble baseline: amber dust, magma red, ice white or emerald green. It drives the thumbnail too.

### 4.3 Recurring devices (all rendered in Remotion)

1. **Earth-globe transition ("the Turn").** Between timeline stages, the last frame zooms out to a small spinning globe on `space-black`. The globe rotates 30–60° while the new stage's planet state fades in. It is 1.2s long, with a sub-bass whoosh. Remotion does it over the tail and head frames of two clips, so it needs no generation.
2. **The T+ clock.** Top-left, JetBrains Mono, amber: `T+ 00:00:01` → `T+ 3 DAYS` → `T+ 10,000 YRS`.
   - A "slam" on each new stage: scale 1.3→1.0 over 6 frames, a 2-frame white flash on the digits, and a metallic tick SFX.
   - Under the clock sits a thin progress bar for a **logarithmic time axis** labeled `SEC · HR · DAY · YR · 1K · 1M`.
3. **The scientist-grade data overlay ("the Readout").** A right-edge panel of 2–4 live metrics that update per stage, for example `SEA LEVEL +70 m`, `DAY LENGTH 24h → 8,766h`, `GLOBAL MEAN °C 14 → 31`, `CO₂ 420 ppm`.
   - Each metric carries a tiny source tag, for example `src: USGS`.
   - Numbers count up with ease-out.
   - Speculative values get a small `MODEL EST.` badge in `data-grey`.
4. **Premise card** (0:03–0:05): white text, "WHAT IF THE MOON DISAPPEARED?", with the Earthlines mark small in a corner.
5. **"Real vs. Speculative" chip.** A small corner badge, `REAL SCIENCE` in cyan or `SPECULATIVE` in amber, on every stage. It is central to compliance (§9).

**Lighting:**
- Physically plausible single-sun key.
- Golden hour and twilight for emotion; noon for scientific clarity.
- The terminator line (day/night boundary) is the channel's signature light.
- Night side with city lights marks human presence. Lights going out marks collapse.

**Do:**
- Keep the planet's axis and rotation consistent.
- Keep scale anchors in every ground shot.
- Slow down for awe.
- Use real place silhouettes only when the script needs them (see §9 labeling).
- End each stage on a composition that the next segment can continue.

**Don't:**
- Put generated on-screen text or numbers in video. The HUD and captions are Remotion only.
- Show recognizable real people, gore, bodies or panicking crowds in close-up.
- Show real brand logos.
- Put a Moon in shots that are supposed to have no Moon. Check continuity.
- Use lens flares on orbital shots; space has no atmosphere to scatter them.
- Let Earth rotate backward.

### 5.2 Prompt templates

```
continuously from frame start to frame end. Single-shot, no camera movement.
```

### 5.3 Continuity rules across stitched segments

1. **Stage Card per timeline stage.** It is a JSON block stored with the script and injected into every prompt for that stage. Its fields are `moon`, `ice_extent`, `ocean_color`, `cloud_regime`, `vegetation`, `night_lights`, `sky_color`, `sun_position`, `palette_preset` and `lens`. **Every segment in a stage uses the identical Stage Card text.**
2. **Stage keyframe still.** Generate one still per stage (orbital) and pass it as `image_1`. Additional references (≤30 total) can hold a style frame and a previous-stage frame for gradual change.
3. **Seamless chaining inside a section.** For a section over 30s, the app splits it into ceil(sec/30) generations and uses the last frame of segment N as the keyframe of segment N+1. Rules:
   - Each segment prompt describes **motion already in progress** ("continues the slow push…", "the wave keeps advancing…").
   - Never write start or stop verbs ("begins", "ends", "cut to").
   - Keep direction, speed, lens, palette and time of day identical.
   - Put visual change (for example the water level rising) as a *continuous gradient* across segments, never as a jump.
4. **Split sizing.** Balance segments evenly (75s → 25+25+25) rather than 30+30+15, so no segment carries a rushed end. Minimum 4s.
5. **Cinema versus exact continuity.** Cinema Studio treats the keyframe as a *reference*, so small drifts can occur at seams. Mask them with the HUD slam or a narration beat, or use **Seedance 2.5 i2v** where frame-exact continuity matters (5.4).
6. **Between sections**, the default is the **Earth-globe transition** (Remotion). **Deliberate hard cuts** (smash cuts) are reserved for (a) the premise moment, (b) disaster onset and (c) the twist. Every idea's stitch plan names them.
7. **Planet-state monotonicity.** A later stage can never look "less changed" than an earlier one unless the script says it recovers. The QA checklist compares stage keyframes side by side.
8. **Moon, sun and axis audit.** QA checks every orbital shot for: Moon presence matching the Stage Card, sun direction and rotation direction.

### 5.4 When to use Seedance 2.5
Use **Seedance 2.5 i2v** (`image_url` as the literal first frame, optional `end_image_url`, 4–30s, 720p) when the shot must honor a **designed still exactly**:
- **Precise planet diagrams.** Tilt angle, orbital geometry, Moon distance to scale, Roche-limit ring formation.
- **Maps and coastlines.** Today's coastline → +70 m coastline, using both start and end frames for a controlled morph.
- **Supercontinent layouts.** Pangaea Proxima/Ultima from a designed paleogeography still.
- **Stage-to-stage morphs.** The Stage N keyframe goes to the Stage N+1 keyframe, as a physically "grown" transition instead of the globe wipe.
- **Seams that must be invisible,** for example the chained continuation of a hero shot.

Keep Cinema Studio for everything cinematic and free-form: surface disasters, aftermath and ground reactions. Seedance t2v is a fallback if Cinema rejects a prompt.

## 9. Compliance and accuracy

### 9.1 YouTube "altered or synthetic content" label: our policy
YouTube requires disclosure when *realistic* content could be mistaken for a real person, place, scene or event. Its examples explicitly include:
- **"altering footage of real events or places, such as making it appear as if a real building caught fire"**
- **"generating realistic scenes that show a realistic depiction of fictional major events, like a tornado moving toward a real town."**

Disclosure is *not* required for clearly unrealistic or animated content, or for AI use in scripts, voice or productivity. Sources: https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/ and https://influencermarketinghub.com/ai-disclosure-rules/.

**Why "no label needed for hypotheticals" is only partly right for us:** our imagery is photoreal, and many scenes show realistic disasters. So:

- **Toggle "Altered content" ON** when a video contains either:
  - (a) any recognizable real place shown altered or damaged (Miami underwater, a real coastline flooding, Yellowstone erupting), or
  - (b) a realistic depiction of a disaster type that really happens (tsunami, eruption, wildfire, hurricane) that a viewer could clip out of context.
  In practice that is **most episodes**. The label is low-cost and protects the channel.
- **The toggle can stay OFF** for clearly impossible cosmic scenes with no real location that no one could mistake for news: Earth with rings, a planet with no Moon seen from orbit, Earth in 1 billion years. The speculative framing, premise card and SPECULATIVE chip already make the fiction obvious. **If in doubt, turn it on.**
- **Never** make fake news framing: no fake broadcast chyrons, no real anchors, no "BREAKING" styling, no real dates presented as events that happened.
- **TikTok and Instagram:** apply the platform AI-generated label on every post.

### 9.2 Accuracy rules
1. **Mark every stage as real or speculative.** `REAL SCIENCE` means established physics, measured data or peer-reviewed models. `SPECULATIVE` means our extrapolation.
2. **Never misstate real climate projections.** The all-ice-melt scenario (~70 m, USGS: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/glaciers-and-icecaps) takes **thousands of years**. The IPCC AR6 likely range for 2100 is **0.28–0.55 m (SSP1-1.9) to 0.63–1.01 m (SSP5-8.5)** (https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/figures/figure-3-4/). Say so explicitly on screen.
3. **Real hazards get real odds.** For example, Yellowstone's caldera-forming eruption probability is about 1 in 730,000 per year (USGS: https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/yellowstone/questions-about-supervolcanoes).
4. **Cite 3–6 sources in every description.** Put NASA, USGS, NOAA, IPCC and peer-reviewed papers first, with a "How we built this simulation" note.
5. **Script QA gate.** A fact-check pass by an LLM plus human review against the "Science grounding notes" for each idea. Flag any number without a source as `MODEL EST.`
6. **Audience 12+.** No gore and no graphic death. Human loss is conveyed through empty cities and lights going out. Don't mark content "Made for Kids" (the audience is 12–40).
7. **Inauthentic-content defense.** Each episode needs:
   - a unique research brief,
   - a unique stage structure,
   - custom data overlays,
   - human editorial review and sign-off,
   - a cadence that does not look mass-produced (≤1 long-form a week initially).

   Keep a production log of human decisions per video (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).
8. **Music and footage rights.** Use only licensed music. Use no NASA imagery in thumbnails without checking NASA media guidelines [U]. Use no real brand logos.
