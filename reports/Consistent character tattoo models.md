# Composite the Tattoos, Generate the Man

No image model available in October 2026, including Nano Banana Pro and Higgsfield's Soul ID or AI Influencer, will redraw thin-line white geometric tattoos identically across 12 views. The only reliable way to get identical tattoos is to stop asking the model to draw them. Let **Nano Banana Pro** generate the man, his pose, the scar and the robotic eye from a fixed reference pack. Then paste one master tattoo design onto every view with the studio's local pixel compositing (mask, warp, blend). Between the two options you named, **Nano Banana Pro is clearly the better choice**. Higgsfield itself promises only "clearly the same person," **not pixel-identical** output. Its Soul ID makes no claim about tattoos or scars, AI Influencer has no tattoo or scar settings, and the Higgsfield API does not offer Nano Banana Pro at all. Among generators, Nano Banana Pro and the two-day-old **Nano Banana 2.1** lead for keeping a recurring photoreal person consistent. **FLUX.2 [pro]**, already wired into the studio, is the strongest challenger on tattoo retention specifically. The full workflow below costs roughly **$15–22 in generation fees** for the whole sheet, plus about 20–40 minutes of compositing per view. The scar and robotic eye are large, distinctive features that models generally hold well. The main risk with them is that the model **swaps them to the wrong side**, and a written side rule plus a per-view check fixes that.

## Every generator redraws fine lines, by design

What you are seeing on the sheet is expected behavior, not a mistake in setup. A Nano Banana storyboarding guide lists it as a known limitation: "Small details like jewelry patterns or **tattoo specifics may vary**." It also warns of "cumulative drift" when images are made by editing earlier outputs ([Flowith](https://flowith.io/blog/nano-banana-consistent-characters-storyboard/)). Higgsfield's own Soul ID documentation promises results that are "clearly the same person" but **not pixel-identical**. That page never mentions tattoos, scars or body marks ([Higgsfield Help Center](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/ai-models/how-do-i-create-and-use-a-soul-id-character)). An academic multi-reference benchmark from July 2026 found that state-of-the-art models still frequently fail "to preserve reference appearances" ([arXiv MIBE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01383)). No public leaderboard measures tattoo, scar or left/right fidelity for any model. The closest ranking, Artificial Analysis's image-editing arena, scores general edit quality, not whether the character stays the same ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/editing)).

Only one independent 2026 test scored tattoo retention directly. A single tester placed a man with a cheek rose and an arm sunflower into a new scene. **FLUX.2 and gpt-image-2 kept both tattoos**. Gemini 3.1 Flash (Nano Banana 2) turned the rose into "a more abstract teardrop" and added an extra sunflower to the neck. Runway Gen-4 produced "strange, abstract shapes" ([techstackups](https://techstackups.com/comparisons/gemini-vs-openai-vs-flux-vs-runway-character-consistency-may-2026/)). Those tattoos were bold, colored and simple. Your character has thin white geometric linework, which is the hardest case. It is high-detail, low-contrast against skin, and defined by exact angles and line counts, so models reproduce it plausibly rather than exactly. A Google developer-forum user also reports that Nano Banana Pro appears to shrink reference images so aggressively that it "can't replicate fine details." That is a single report, but it fits what you have observed ([Google AI Dev forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-3-0-pro-image-preview-inconsistent-performance-with-multiple-reference-images-since-3-1-launch/128648)).

Asymmetric features carry a separate risk: the model may put them on the wrong side. GPT Image 2 showed **left/right limb confusion** in a multi-panel sprite-sheet workflow ([Linocut](https://linocut.ai/blogs/multi-reference-ai-image-models/)). Guides also warn that a mirrored detail spreads to later images if you generate each new image from the previous output instead of from the original references ([Flowith](https://flowith.io/blog/nano-banana-consistent-characters-storyboard/)). The scar and the robotic eye are large and distinctive, so expect them to survive in most generations. In a front view, though, the subject's left eye appears on the viewer's right, and that is exactly where models get confused. The way around this is the approach professional character work converges on: **the AI generates the body, and the tattoo comes from one master design applied in post**. This is the only method that guarantees identical line geometry ([CapCut workflow](https://www.capcut.com/create/character-consistency-in-ai-images-workflow); [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/how-to-create-a-character-reference-sheet-with-ai/)).

## Nano Banana Pro beats Higgsfield for this sheet

Higgsfield offers two character tools, and neither fits this job. **Soul ID** is a trained identity. It needs 20 or more photos (up to 80 in the app, 1–100 through the API), takes "a few minutes" to train, and focuses on the face. The trained identity cannot be exported as a file ([Higgsfield Help Center](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/ai-models/how-do-i-create-and-use-a-soul-id-character)). Training it on your current 12 views would be counterproductive: they show 12 different versions of the tattoos, and Soul ID would learn that inconsistency. **AI Influencer** builds a new character from settings, a prompt or one photo. Creating or editing a character costs 1.12 credits. Its skin options cover freckles, vitiligo and birthmarks but **no tattoos or scars** ([Higgsfield AI Influencer](https://www.higgsfield.ai/ai-influencer)). It is designed to invent a persona, not to lock in an existing design.

Higgsfield's developer API, launched in September 2026, has real strengths. It is pay-as-you-go from a $5 top-up with no subscription ([Higgsfield Help Center: API](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/integrations/what-is-the-higgsfield-api)). It exposes Soul ID training at `POST /v1/custom-references` ([Higgsfield docs](https://docs.higgsfield.ai/docs/models/soul-id/create-character.md)). Soul 2 and Soul Cinema images start at **$0.0032 each**, possibly a launch-promotion rate ([Higgsfield blog](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/higgsfield-api)). However, the API's image catalog lists Soul, Soul V2, Soul Cinema, Grok Image 2.0, Recraft, Qwen Image 3, Ideogram 4.0 and others. It does **not include Nano Banana Pro, FLUX or Seedream**, and it has **no AI Influencer endpoint** ([Higgsfield docs: Image Generation](https://docs.higgsfield.ai/docs/models/image-generation.md)). The Higgsfield web app does offer Nano Banana Pro, at about 2 credits per standard image and 4 at 4K ([Krea blog](https://www.krea.ai/blog/higgsfield-pricing-explained-2026-unlimited-credits-and-real-monthly-costs)). That is the same model you already call through fal, with no automation. Higgsfield becomes worthwhile later, as a cheap way to produce many images of the face, or to carry the character into Seedance video, where a trained Soul ID appears automatically as an "Element." It does not help with getting the tattoos identical.

Two caveats apply to this ranking. First, the techstackups result favoring FLUX.2 comes from a single tester using colored tattoos, so treat it as a reason to test FLUX.2, not as proof. Second, the leaderboard measures general edit quality, not identity. That is why GPT Image 2.5 ranks fifth here despite its top score: the reported left/right weakness matters a great deal for a one-sided scar and eye. A **training route** is also available. A character LoRA on fal costs about **$2 per 1,000 steps** on Qwen-Image or **$6.40–8** on FLUX.2 [dev], and FLUX.2 inference with a LoRA costs $0.021 per megapixel ([fal flux-2-trainer](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/flux-2-trainer/llms.txt); [fal qwen-image-trainer](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/qwen-image-trainer/llms.txt); [fal flux-2/lora](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/flux-2/lora)). A LoRA only pays off if you will need hundreds of images, not for one 12-view sheet.

## A composite-first workflow locks the tattoos

The workflow uses each tool for what it does reliably. The generator handles identity, pose, lighting, scar and eye. The local compositor handles the tattoo lines. Guides consistently give the same three rules. Supply close-up references for small identity details, because a full-body view cannot show them at a usable size ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/how-to-create-a-character-reference-sheet-with-ai/)). Name distinctive marks as "anchor points" in every prompt ([Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/ai-character-consistency-guide)). Fix local defects with a local correction rather than regenerating the whole image ([CapCut workflow](https://www.capcut.com/create/character-consistency-in-ai-images-workflow)). The compositing recipe adapts the standard displacement-map tattoo technique to white ink ([Planet Photoshop](https://planetphotoshop.com/digital-tattoos.html); [Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/products/photoshop/displacement-map.html)). In the table, "left" and "right" always mean the character's own left and right.

| Step | What the operator does | Tool and cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Write the spec | Write one fixed text block and paste it into every prompt. It covers: white thin-line geometric tattoos, healed, covering the whole neck and the backs of both hands; a scar over his LEFT eye; a robotic LEFT eye. Add "his LEFT = viewer's right in front views." Swap LEFT for RIGHT if the character is the other way round. | Free |
| 2. Make the master plates | Generate dedicated 4K close-ups: neck (front and side), back of left hand, back of right hand, and a face close-up showing the scar and robotic eye. Make about 4 variants of each and approve one per area. These approved images are now the official design. | `make_character_image`, Nano Banana Pro 4K: about 5 plates × 4 × $0.30 ≈ **$6** |
| 3. Turn the plates into stencils | Extract the tattoo lines from each approved plate into a flat white-on-transparent layer, using high-pass/threshold and cleanup in local compositing, or have an artist trace them. Label each stencil with its side. | Local compositing, free |
| 4. Regenerate the 12 views | For every view, start fresh from the same 6 references: face front, full-body front, scar/eye close-up, neck plate, left-hand plate, right-hand plate. Never generate a view by editing the previous view. Make 4 variants per view and choose based on pose, face, and which side the scar and eye are on. Ignore tattoo accuracy at this stage. | Nano Banana Pro 2K: 12 × 4 × $0.15 = **$7.20** (4K: $14.40) |
| 5. Composite the tattoos | Mask each visible tattoo area. Warp the stencil onto it: a mesh or arc warp around the neck, separate small pieces for each finger segment, and anchor points at the jaw angle, collarbone and wrist. Displace with a slightly blurred grayscale copy of the skin. Blend with Screen/Lighten at **50–80% opacity**, tinted ivory rather than pure white, with a 0.5–1 px blur, and masked by skin brightness so pores show through. Hide the tattoo wherever hair, clothing or the other hand covers it. | Local compositing, about 20–40 min per view, free |
| 6. Optional blending pass | Run Nano Banana Pro edit on the composited view with the instruction "only make the tattoo look healed into the skin; do not change any lines." Compare the result against the stencil. If any line moved, keep the step-5 version instead. | 12 × $0.15 = **$1.80** |
| 7. Check the full set | Lay all 12 views side by side. Overlay the stencil to check line count and angles. Confirm that the scar and robotic eye sit on the correct side in every view where they are visible: both face profiles, face smile, and the front and three-quarter torso views. | Free |
| 8. Lock the library | Save the 12 final views plus the 5 plates as the character's reference library. Any future hero shot starts from these references and goes through step 5 again. | Free |

The full run costs about **$15** with the views made at 2K, or about **$22** at 4K, plus one or two operator days for compositing. Spend the compositing effort where it shows. The neck tattoo is large in the face and torso views and needs a careful warp there. In the four full-body views, a hand covers only a few dozen pixels, so a simpler paste will hold up. Hands are the hardest region because every finger segment is its own plane and the model re-poses fingers freely. For each hand, pick the variant with the cleanest, flattest hand before you start compositing. If you want a quick tie-break between generators before the full run, try steps 2 and 4 on two views (torso front and face profile) with **Nano Banana 2.1** and **FLUX.2 [pro]**. That costs under $3. Whichever model gives better skin and correct sides needs the least compositing cleanup, but the tattoo lines come from step 5 either way.

## Conclusion

The question changes from "which model draws the tattoo the same every time" to "which model gives the cleanest base for a tattoo you apply yourself." No current model answers the first. Nano Banana Pro answers the second well, and FLUX.2 [pro] and Nano Banana 2.1 are worth a cheap test against it. This also means the master stencils, not the model, become the character's real identity record. They outlast model upgrades, they transfer to any future generator or to video frames, and they make the sheet auditable: anyone can overlay the stencil and confirm the lines match.

Several things remain unknown. No one has published a controlled test of white-ink fine-line tattoos, scar side or robotic-eye placement on any model. Nano Banana 2.1's fal price and real-world consistency are two days old and unverified. Higgsfield's Soul ID training price is not published. Fal prices change often and should be re-checked before budgeting. The one outcome you can count on is that the composited tattoo lines will be identical in every view.
