# Global-History ET Channel — Setup Pack

Working concept: a humanoid alien field researcher covers global history/events
from an extraterrestrial perspective. Authoritative, intellectual, educational
voice; semi-educational, semi-humor. This pack is paste-ready: character image
prompts → your image generator → the app's Avatar presenter card; the tone
block → Project instructions; the research links → "study the winners" /
video-intel review.

---

## 1. Channel tone

**Voice:** Authoritative, intellectual, dryly amused. A senior researcher
delivering findings to an off-world audience — never zany, never mugging. The
comedy comes from *precise, deadpan description of genuinely absurd human
behavior*, not from jokes told at anyone's expense.

**Register rules (paste into Project instructions):**

> The narrator is an alien field researcher filing numbered broadcasts about
> Earth's history for an off-world scientific audience. Voice: authoritative,
> intellectual, dry, faintly baffled. Rules: humans are "the species"; episodes
> are "transmissions" or "field reports" with numbers; an unseen skeptical
> supervisor recurs (reports get rejected, budgets get questioned); the
> narrator finds human cooperation and art genuinely moving, and human
> paperwork, borders, and currency incomprehensible; never breaks character;
> hedges contested facts as "human records claim…". Humor = deadpan precision
> about absurd behavior. Never mock victims, tragedy, or specific living
> people. Warmth underneath: the alien is slowly, reluctantly, becoming fond
> of the species.

**Emotional formula per episode:** 70% real history told cleanly · 20% deadpan
alien reframing · 10% genuine wonder (the beat where the alien admits the
species did something remarkable — this is what makes the character loved
rather than merely funny).

## 4. Three master script prompt templates

Use as the project's script template (Settings → template editor) or paste per
video. Replace `[TOPIC]` / bracketed slots. All three assume the character
bible from §1 is in Project instructions (it injects automatically).

### Template 1 — "Field Report" (default; 6–10 min documentary)

> Write transmission #[N] of the alien field researcher's broadcast series,
> covering [TOPIC]. Open with the researcher stating the single most absurd
> or improbable fact about [TOPIC] as a finding that the supervisor initially
> refused to accept — a genuine contradiction with one specific number, date,
> or named detail in the first two sentences. Structure as a field report:
> (1) what the species did, told as clean, accurate narrative history with
> concrete dates, figures, and named humans; (2) the researcher's analysis of
> WHY the species did it, where deadpan alien logic exposes the absurdity;
> (3) the consequence humans rarely discuss, foreshadowed since the open.
> Every 30–45 seconds, open a new question the next section must answer. One
> genuine moment of admiration for the species, placed just before the
> biggest revelation. Close with the report's official conclusion (one sharp
> insight reframing the whole event), a one-line note from the supervisor,
> and a teaser for the next transmission's subject.

### Template 2 — "The Hearing" (contested topics; 8–12 min; highest drama ceiling)

> Write transmission #[N] as the alien researcher DEFENDING a controversial
> finding about [TOPIC] before an off-world review board (voiced only through
> the researcher's responses — "You ask how a single flower bankrupted a
> nation. I will show you."). Open mid-defense with the researcher's most
> indefensible-sounding claim about [TOPIC], stated flatly with one specific
> figure. Structure as evidence exhibits: Exhibit A, B, C — each a real
> historical episode with dates, numbers, and named humans, each ending on
> the board's next objection (the open loop). The researcher grows more
> passionate as evidence mounts; the final exhibit is the emotional one where
> the species' behavior, while absurd, reveals something the board cannot
> dismiss. Close with the board's verdict — always grudging, always funny —
> and the researcher's quiet personal note that contradicts it slightly.

### Template 3 — "The Ranking" (listicle/countdown; 8–15 min; strongest mid-retention architecture)

> Write transmission #[N] as the alien researcher's official ranking of
> [CATEGORY — e.g., "the species' five strangest border disputes"], counted
> DOWN from #[X] to #1. Open by naming the ranking's criteria in alien
> bureaucratic language and asserting that #1 required the creation of a new
> classification category — do not reveal it. Each entry: the real history
> in 60–90 seconds with one unforgettable specific detail, then the
> researcher's one-line filed assessment. Escalate genuinely — every entry
> must out-absurd the last, and reference the pending #1 at entries #3 and
> #2. #1 gets double length and ends with the researcher's admission that
> the species cannot be modeled. Close with which rankings the supervisor
> disputed, and next transmission's teaser.

## 6. Critical channel/video notes

- **One presenter image, forever.** Character consistency = the same uploaded
  image driving every avatar shot. Redesigns are channel reboots; don't.
- **The face is the CTR asset.** Thumbnail formula: alien face (consistent
  angle) + one historical object/painting + ≤4 words. Silhouette must read
  at 120px.
- **Never break character** — in videos, descriptions, pinned comments, or
  community posts. The bit compounds only if it's total.
- **Accuracy is load-bearing.** The comedy earns trust only if the history is
  right. Keep the fact-check advisory notes visible in review; use "human
  records claim…" for anything contested. History audiences punish errors
  in comments — and the algorithm reads the resulting engagement as negative.
- **Tragedy guardrail:** wars/famines are coverable (WWI is idea #2), but the
  alien's deadpan aims at *decisions and systems*, never at suffering. This is
  both ethics and monetization (advertiser-safety on history content).
- **AI disclosure:** tick YouTube's synthetic/altered-content disclosure on
  upload; the pipeline's publish metadata should carry it by default.
- **Cost per episode (current stack):** ~$0.05 script + ~$0.40 VO + ~$1–2
  stills/b-roll + avatar hook/close ~$1.50–3 (Kling) ≈ **$3–6 per long-form**,
  under $1 per short. Run the first 3 pilots before any premium (OmniHuman)
  hero shots.
- **The supervisor is the franchise.** The unseen skeptical supervisor is the
  engagement engine — end-card notes, pinned comments "from the supervisor,"
  community polls ("the supervisor demands to know which report to file
  next"). Fans will address comments to them within weeks.
- **Measure with the outcome loop.** File the first 5 transmissions, then ask
  the app "how are my videos performing?" — promote whichever format (Report
  / Hearing / Ranking) wins into the default template.
